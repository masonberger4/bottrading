`entries.py`, on the "week rule off" run (2,897 trades). R = result divided by the trade's own risk.

- Direction: no setup shows a measurable move in the trade's direction 30 or 60 minutes later or at the close.
- Timing: every setup hits its stop before a 1R, 2R or 3R target more often than a coin flip would. Give the same
  entries a stop three times wider and they become an exact coin flip (0.00 R before costs).
- So the entries carry no directional information, and the tight stops add a loss on top. PR #75 confirmed the
  entry-bar stops are real: the stop is often under half a 5m bar. Wider stops remove that loss but leave zero edge,
  which matches the earlier wider-stop switch tests (same or worse).

**What separates winners from losers.** 276 feature splits tested per setup (bias columns, side, weekday, news,
confluence count, entry time, risk size, range, and so on), found on 2019-22 and checked on 2023-26:
31 found (13 expected by chance), 3 confirmed (1.6 expected by chance):

- AM with the reaction bias positive (`b_react = 1`): -0.02 R vs -0.49 R for the rest, same direction on all three
  markets. It moves AM from losing to break-even, not to profit.
- Last hour against the premium / discount bias: -0.58 R vs +0.09 R for the rest (88 vs 127 trades).

Neither is strong enough to trade on; both are worth a real run as switches.

## 3. What this means and suggested next steps (Mase's call)

The engine's entries, as built, are coin flips on these three markets. More filter or stop switches will not change
that. Suggested order:

1. Check the engine against ICT's own trades (thread "Check the engine against ICT's video trades"): does the engine
   take the trades he takes, at his prices? If not, the gap is in how the entry rules were coded, and that is where
   the work is.
2. Run the entries on 1m bars. Stops are smaller than a 5m bar, so 5m cannot say what happened inside the entry bar.
   Needs the 1m files copied to the cloud (about 900 MB) and about 5x the run time.
3. Price-check the overnight-gap scalp trades, then get more of them if they hold up.
4. Keep the weekly-rule default on hold; no reading is profitable.

## Summary
Only two institutional ways a market turns: (1) the breaker swing point (stop run past an old high/low into a key level, then reversal) and (2) the failure swing (price fails to retest/overtake the key level). For both, the safe entry is the retest of the short-term market structure breaking point after the shift, with the stop beyond the swing that already ran (or failed at) the liquidity. The breaker is preferred (deepest discount / highest premium, "two-chance setup"); the failure swing is the fallback when the breaker does not come.

## Rules
- Market turns only one of two ways: breaker swing point (stop run) or failure swing. A seller sells either at/above an old high or on a retracement to a market-structure break; mirror for buyers. ~01:46, ~30:37-31:36 [new]
- Breaker swing point (bearish): price rallies to or just short of a key level (bearish OB, mitigation/breaker, old high, FVG/liquidity void) and fails to react; expect one more drive higher that takes the short-term high (buy stops) and reaches the level, then reversal. ~03:15-05:26 [chart] [new as a named setup; consistent with Ep 05 breaker and Ep 08 turtle soup]
- Trigger: once price breaks the short-term low between the old high and the new higher high (MSS breaking point), sell the retest of that breaking point. ~09:46-11:03 [chart] [new]
- Stop for that short: above the new high (or right at it); the buy stops there were already run, so the market has no reason to return. ~11:06-11:24, ~26:57-27:22 [chart] [new]
- Bullish mirror: short-term low forms at or just above support, one more drive takes it (sell stops) into the support level (bullish OB, unfilled void, old low); buy the retest of the short-term high that gets broken; stop below the sweep low. ~13:06-16:24 [chart] [new]
- Retest may not reach the exact breaking point: price can turn at a nearer order block formed during the break, or not retrace at all (missed trade). ~16:58-17:24 [new]
- Want an immediate, aggressive response away from the swept level; price should not linger beyond the old low/high. ~14:40-14:50 [new]
- Old high/low used for the stop run has no age limit (can be months old); ideal is a recent one (days to a week). ~13:24-13:51 [new]
- Key levels (OB, breaker, mitigation, FVG, liquidity void) must already be drawn before price arrives, or the pattern cannot be anticipated. ~07:35-07:50, ~08:58-09:23 [new]
- First stop run in an intermediate-term price swing is the ideal one; smart money unseats aggressive trailing stops. ~10:20 slide point 3 [chart] [new]
- Failure swing (bearish): price trades through or just touches a key level, breaks down, retraces, but cannot make another pass to it (no higher high). Entry: on the retest of the initial key level, or after the short-term low (MSS breaking point) breaks and is retested. ~19:36-20:10, ~24:40 slide [chart] [new]
- Failure swing stop: above the failed short-term high (the area where buy stops were already run). ~24:44-25:07 [chart] [new]
- Failure-swing bullish mirror: higher low that holds above support, break of the short-term high, buy the retest; stop below the low. ~25:10-25:40, ~29:20 [chart] [new]
- Between the first reaction and the break, the retracement is a "gray area": do not assume which pattern forms; wait for the structure break if unsure. ~23:35-24:11 [new]

## Definitions
- Swing high (Larry Williams): three-candle pattern, a high with lower highs either side; swing low mirror. Not what this lesson is about. ~00:29-00:54
- Institutional swing point: a turning point that comes from manipulation, either a stop run (breaker) or a failure swing. ~01:46 [new]
- Breaker swing point: higher high taking the short-term high then breakdown through the short-term low between them; the trapped shorts' break is the "breaker". ~05:26-05:52 [chart]
- Failure swing: second leg fails to retest/overtake the key level, then breaks structure. ~19:36 [chart]
- Market structure breaking point: the short-term low (sells) / high (buys) whose break confirms the turn; retest of it is the entry. ~09:50 [chart]

## Summary
An "ideal" seasonal is one where a currency's seasonal and the dollar index's seasonal are diametrically opposed over the same window. Main one: DXY tends to top in March and fall into May, while AUD, NZD, GBP, CAD and JPY bottom in March-April and rally into May. The EUR/CHF ideal is a June-July low (DXY summer high). Ends with a daily routine: review the macro/seasonal calendar every morning. Index-futures relevance is limited to the DXY calendar and the routine.

## Rules
- Ideal seasonal = the currency future's seasonal and the DXY future's seasonal move opposite in the same window; only the most obvious such windows are used for long-term trades ~1:09-1:26, ~3:52-4:08 [new] [chart]
- Take an ideal seasonal only when the underlying HTF market already leans that way (primary bull trend or a suspected long-term low); otherwise it is just a test ~2:53-3:06, ~4:14-4:30, ~7:22-7:40 [new]
- You need only one side in trend: either DXY bearish or the counter-currency bullish; DXY can be in consolidation ~11:13-12:15 [new]
- Pair construction decides direction: base currency rally = pair up (AUDUSD); USD base = pair down (USDJPY, USDCAD sells when DXY seasonally weak) ~2:24-2:50, ~9:27-9:50, ~10:29-10:45 (FX-only)
- A tendency confirmed on a long sample and a recent sample (e.g. GBP 40y and 15y) is stronger ~6:29-7:11 [new]
- Seasonals are road maps, not certainties; they will not fire every year ~0:37-0:53, ~1:32-1:40
- Routine: write the seasonal windows into a calendar ahead of time; start every trading day with a macro review ("are we in a window for a sustainable move?"); keep things-to-watch for this month and next ~12:27-13:19 [new]
- Seasonals will be reused later for swing and short-term trading ~13:30-13:41

# cc10_005 ICT Mentorship Core Content - Month 10 - Open Interest Secrets & Smart Money Footprints (qRYkTWePXF0)
June 2017 Core Content (commodity lesson 5). Markets: currency futures (British pound, Euro FX on IMM), general commodities. Timeframes: monthly, weekly, daily + open interest panels. ~20 min. Paper-trading framing only.

## Summary
Open interest (OI) read two ways: as trend/consolidation strength, and as the footprint of commercial hedgers. ICT's twist on Larry Williams: OI only matters when price is consolidating at a higher-timeframe PD array, and compared with OI's multi-year seasonal average. This is a swing/position filter, not a day-trade trigger.

## Rules
- OI = total outstanding contracts at end of each day; count one side only (buyer + seller = one contract) ~00:02:30 [new]
- Trend reading (Williams): uptrend + rising OI = bullish; downtrend + rising OI = bearish; uptrend + falling OI = bearish; downtrend + falling OI = bullish ("supply of losers exhausted") ~00:03:34-00:05:20 [new]
- Consolidation reading: range + rising OI = bearish (commercials shorting into speculators' longs); range + falling OI = bullish (commercials covering shorts) ~00:05:20-00:07:39 [chart] [new]
- Only act on it at a higher-timeframe level: falling OI while consolidating at HTF support / discount array -> expect upswing; rising OI while consolidating at HTF resistance / premium array -> expect downswing ~00:07:39-00:08:32 [new]
- Confirm with COT: commercials net long (bullish case) / net short or increasing shorts (bearish case) ~00:06:58, ~00:14:28 [new]
- Compare actual OI to its multi-year seasonal average line; the signal is OI dropping below the average (bullish at support) or above it (bearish at resistance), not just the direction of OI ~00:11:45-00:13:21 [chart] [new]
- Contract roll (Mar/Jun/Sep/Dec) does not by itself explain OI changes; positions roll one-for-one ~00:10:14-00:11:31 [new]
- Use: swing/position bias "a couple times a year"; once aligned, day trade in that direction, e.g. days where the open is near the low, or a weekly-range expansion to a higher Friday close vs the week's open ~00:18:27-00:19:08 [new]

## Trade examples
- British pound futures (Mar 2011 contract chart), 2010: monthly bullish OBs (2009 and early 2010), price retraces to weekly old bullish breaker, ~1.53 support (blue line) [chart]; Aug-Sep 2010 consolidation (green box) with OI falling below its seasonal average, commercials net long -> ~800 pips in under 2 months ~00:13:34-00:15:37.
- Euro FX (Mar 2011 contract), 2010: rally from early-2010 low, retrace from 1.33 to weekly bullish OB at 1.26 (last two down-close weekly candles; OB high marked); Aug-Sep 2010 daily range ~1.265-1.29 [chart]; OI drops well below seasonal average, commercials net long -> rally to ~1.42, ~1500 pips in under 2 months ~00:15:55-00:18:13.

## Definitions
- Open interest: total outstanding contracts held at end of day; measures money flow in, while volume measures pressure/intensity ~00:02:30
- Seasonal OI average: dotted multi-year average OI line (CRB Trader / pricecharts.com plot it); actual OI = solid line ~00:09:27 [chart]
- Bullish OB (weekly): last two down-close candles; level = their high ~00:16:25

## Seen only on the charts
- Seasonal chart (May 2010-Jan 2011): OI dips at mid-June, mid-Sep, mid-Dec (contract roll); actual OI ran above average Jun-Sep, then fell below it after the Sep roll [chart].
- Data sources named: barchart.com total OI, CRB Trader, pricecharts.com.

## Open questions
- No numeric threshold for "well below" the seasonal average.
- Index-futures use: OI is end-of-day only, so at best a daily/weekly bias filter for ES; not clear ICT would apply it to stock indices (he cites S&P only as a roll example).

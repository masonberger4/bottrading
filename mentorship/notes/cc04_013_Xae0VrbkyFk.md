## Summary
Indicators carry no information about where orders sit. A classic (type 1) bearish divergence at a
high with nearby buy stops is a retail trap: price dips into a bullish order block / below equal lows,
prints a higher low while the stochastic makes a lower low (type 2 "hidden" bullish divergence), then
runs the buy stops above the highs. Use indicators only as a contrarian read of retail sentiment.

## Rules
- Never trade off an indicator; at most use it to see what retail expects and fade it when
  institutional order flow (liquidity, OBs) points the other way ~0:49-1:40, ~21:23-21:50 [new]
- Type 1 divergence: higher high in price, lower high in momentum (bearish); mirror for bullish ~2:39-2:59
- Type 2 (hidden, trend-following): higher low in price with a lower low in momentum = bullish
  continuation; mirror for bearish ~3:01-4:11, ~13:53-14:08 [new]
- A high with buy stops just above it will be run; a bearish divergence there is a trap ~6:09-6:21, ~7:29-7:32 [new]
- Expected sequence: retracement takes equal lows (sell stops), dips into the bullish OB, holds about
  its mean threshold, then rallies through the highs ~6:38-7:09, ~11:47-12:12 [chart] [new]
- The stochastic does not need to be oversold for the bullish case ~12:15-12:20
- Bullish OB = last down candle before the up move; use the body (open), not the wick, when the wick
  area has already been traded through several times; the low should hold, not trade below the
  middle of that body ~16:07-17:20 [chart] [new nuance; Ep 03 anchors OBs at the open]
- Fifth condition besides overbought/oversold/divergence: is it time to trade at all? Avoid
  consolidations ~5:25-5:35 [new]

## Definitions
- Divergence phantom: an indicator divergence that looks like a reversal signal but is engineered
  liquidity; price goes the other way ~20:04-20:15
- Market efficiency paradigm: market makers engineer or neutralize liquidity by drawing traders in or
  knocking them out ~1:49-2:14

## Summary
Formal bullish order block (OB) rules: what it is, how it is validated, where to enter, stop and
target. Mirror everything for bearish. Then "liquidity-based bias": when monthly, weekly and daily
agree, only trade OBs in that direction, and use opposite OBs only as profit-taking spots. The
USDX example refines a monthly OB down to weekly and daily levels.

## Rules
- Bullish OB = the lowest down-close candle, with the largest open-to-close range, near a support level (an HTF old low, or an old high price has broken above and is retesting) ~00:00:35, ~00:01:36 [chart] [new] (refines the "last down-close candle" wording)
- Validation: the OB is confirmed only when a later candle trades above the OB candle's high ~00:00:35, ~00:03:36 [chart] [new]
- Entry: after price trades away and comes back to the OB's high. He means the top of the body (the open of the down candle) and wants you focused on bodies, not wicks ~00:01:36, ~00:05:37, ~00:07:38 [chart]. The retrace can even come in the same candle that validated it ~00:03:36 [new]
- Limit entry: add a few pips (~5 in FX) above the OB open to cover the spread; or buy at market as it hits. Price may push deeper into the OB, which is fine ~00:06:38-00:07:38 [new] (FX spread; for futures maybe 1-2 ticks, our reading)
- Mean threshold = 50% of the down candle's BODY (open to close), not wicks. The best OBs don't trade below it at all; a small stab through is tolerable ~00:09:38 [conflict]: mentorship_rules.md (Ep 02) measures the MT from the OB high to low with wicks. The slide itself says "50% of the Orderblock total range" ~00:02:00 [chart], so the slide and his words disagree
- Stop: below the OB's low; he says mainly the low formed by the body's close, sometimes the wick low ~00:10:39 [chart] [new]. After price runs away, the stop can be raised to just below the 50% of the OB ~00:01:36 (slide) [new]
- Target: buy stops above the old high (ERL); take partial or full profit there ~00:08:38, ~00:10:39 [chart]
- Displacement filter: the move away from the OB should be at least 2-3 times the OB's body height before you expect a retracement entry ~00:26:51-00:28:52 [chart] [new] (he says this is the first time he has stated it)
- Consecutive down candles at the low are blended into one OB ~00:31:02 [new]
- If a newer down candle forms higher, the OB level moves up to that candle's open ("higher order block") ~00:27:51, ~00:29:53 [new]
- Liquidity-based bias: monthly, weekly and daily all bearish means 4h and lower retrace higher into a premium. Sell buy-stop raids, bearish OBs, FVGs or liquidity-void fills, targeting below a recent low. Bullish mirror ~00:11:39-00:15:41 [chart] [new]
- Targets: prefer a daily-chart liquidity level (yesterday's, last week's or last month's high/low), ideally with a weekly objective beyond it ~00:13:40, ~00:15:41 [new]
- Monthly drives direction; the daily changes direction most often. Weekly and monthly need much longer to turn ~00:13:40
- Avoid counter-trend OBs: with HTF bullish, don't short at bearish OBs (last up candles); use them only as profit-taking spots ~00:24:49 [new]
- At a profit-taking time of day, an opposing OB may cap the move, so take profit there. When time of day is not in play, ignore it and expect the run through the old high ~00:34:06 [new]
- Refine HTF levels down to lower timeframes (as low as 5m) for smaller stops, but direction comes from monthly/weekly/daily ~00:33:04 [new]
- He sat out the 2016 US election period ~00:32:02

## Definitions
- Bullish OB, validation, entry, risk: see Rules (slide ~00:02:00) [chart]
- Mean threshold (OB): 50% of the down candle's body, open to close ~00:09:38
- Displacement: the price-action sign that institutions are in the move; here a move of at least 2-3 OB heights away ~00:04:37, ~00:27:51
- Liquidity-based bias: monthly/weekly/daily agreement sets the direction; intraday (4h and below) is the retracement to trade ~00:12:40

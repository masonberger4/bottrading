2016-17 Private Mentorship, Month 01, teaching 7 of 8 (slide footer "September 2016").
Market: NZD/USD 4h (forex, MT4 demo, ~Oct-Dec 2015); the rest is line-drawn diagrams.
Timeframes: 4h only, but he says the concept is timeframe-independent. Duration ~25 min.
No trades taken; conceptual lesson.

## Summary
Liquidity = resting buy and sell orders: buy stops above old highs, sell stops below old
lows. Price is drawn back to them. He splits runs to that liquidity into high-resistance
runs (lots of overlapping swings in the way: low odds) and low-resistance runs (fast,
one-sided leg with few retracements: high odds). Trade only toward low-resistance liquidity.

## Rules
- Liquidity = buy orders and sell orders. Shorts entered off a swing high keep their stops
  just above that high (sometimes right at it); longs off a swing low keep stops below it.
  So buy-side liquidity sits above old highs and sell-side below old lows, and price
  tends to return to take them out ~1:09-4:43 [chart]. Same as Ep 01.
- Bias comes from price only, no indicators: price moving away from an old high means
  liquidity rests above it; away from an old low, liquidity rests below ~4:47-5:31.
- High-resistance liquidity run (HRLR): the old high is far above current price, and
  between them sits a series of lower highs/lower lows (many overlapping peaks and troughs).
  Old lows act as resistance and each intermediate high is its own buy-stop pool, so price
  may stop at any of those instead. Least likely condition for longs; avoid it ~5:38-8:14
  [chart] [new].
- An old high protected by an HRLR is usually defended. Generally only a big volatility
  event breaks it: NFP, FOMC, an unexpected black swan ~8:23-8:51 [new].
- Mirror: an old low under a series of higher highs/higher lows is an HRLR for shorts. The
  longer price spends in that overlap, the less likely the old low is reached. Avoid shorts
  there unless there is a big news driver ~8:57-10:57 [chart] [new].
- Low-resistance liquidity run (LRLR), bullish: a fast, one-way drop from an old high
  with few retracements breaks an old low. After price then breaks a short-term high
  (structure shift up), the leg from that short-term high up to the broken low is
  low-resistance ~11:25-12:51 [chart] [new].
- Inside that zone, each new short-term high gets buy stops above it. Every buy signal
  after a retracement should run through the high it retraced from. Repeat until price
  refills the area up to the broken low ~13:04-14:28 [new].
- Once price is back at the broken low (where the fast leg began), anything higher is an
  HRLR again and the odds drop sharply: a natural final target / stop-adding level for
  longs ~13:34-14:38 [new].
- Bearish mirror: consolidation, expansion up breaks a short-term high, quick one-sided
  rally, then a break of an old low. From there down to the broken short-term high (where
  the rally began) is low-resistance; sell every retracement aiming at the short-term lows
  ~15:00-16:59 [chart] [new].
- With an old higher high held (lower highs below it), every rally is an HRLR and every
  short-term low is an LRLR target. Bias is bearish: sell rallies and target short-term or
  intermediate-term lows ~19:22-20:43 [chart] [new]. Mirror for bullish ~21:21-23:38.
- Market-maker framing: buy low, sell to willing buyers above the market. In a bullish
  LRLR, each pullback sets up the next run above an old high ~22:56-23:38.
- Too-clean highs/lows (equal levels) mark buy/sell stops; any small retracement sets up
  another drive through them ~23:53-24:13. Matches Ep 04 / Ep 06.
- A level with lots of price action around it is being defended; trade away from it, in
  the institutional order-flow direction, for low drawdown ~24:23-24:51 [new].

## Definitions
- Liquidity (slide): how quickly a market can be bought or sold without moving its price
  much. ICT's working meaning: resting buy and sell orders ~0:29, ~1:09 [chart].
- High-resistance liquidity run: a move toward an old high/low that has to pass many
  overlapping swings (old lows as resistance, intermediate highs as competing stop pools)
  ~7:34 [new].
- Low-resistance liquidity run: a move inside the range left by a fast, one-sided leg, from
  the structure break back to where the leg started. Few swings in the way ~11:21, ~12:44
  [new].
- Short-term vs intermediate-term vs long-term highs/lows are used but not defined here
  (he mentions "open float" as a later concept) ~3:52, ~18:47.

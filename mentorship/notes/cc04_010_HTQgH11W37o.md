## Summary
A liquidity void is a fast, one-sided leg of long candles that leaves a consolidation. Here it was the 15 Dec drop from ~1.0480 to ~1.0450. Price tends to come back and trade over the whole leg. Before the fill, sell stops under nearby lows get run, and that run is the buy. After the void was filled twice at 1.0476, a small open/close gap appeared on the way down; he sold it with a limit order and targeted the sell stops below equal lows.

## Rules
- A liquidity void is the range covered by long one-sided candles that leave a consolidation (displacement). On a higher TF it can be a single large candle. Expect price to revisit and fully cover that range with opposite-direction price action ~0:58, ~3:00, ~5:35-6:51 [chart] [conflict: mentorship_rules.md (Ep 01) says a liquidity void is an actual gap with no candle overlap; here it is the whole one-sided range, and the true open/close gap is a "common gap"]
- Fill target = the start of the void leg, i.e. the consolidation edge it left from (1.0476 in the example). Full coverage = "balanced", "complete and uniform" delivery ~4:46, ~6:51 [chart] [new]
- No time limit for a fill. Voids can stay open for months or fill within one session; judge from the surrounding price action ~2:17-2:43 [new]

## Definitions
- Liquidity void (slide): a range where one side of market liquidity appears as wide/long one-sided candles; price usually comes back to this "porous" range of contrarian liquidity ~0:58 [chart]
- Price in balance = consolidation / equilibrium. A move out of it caused by smart money = imbalance / displacement ~1:28-2:00
- Common gap: a gap between one candle's close and the next one's open with no price trading in it. Not something you expect price to run away from: it gets closed (used as a sell entry above) ~12:22-12:50 [new]
- A bearish void is a void of buy-side liquidity (only selling took place) ~4:20-4:46

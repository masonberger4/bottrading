# cc12_001 ICT Mentorship Core Content - Month 12 - Long Term Top Down Analysis (eIdnsgq-G7A)
Recorded August 2017 / example: AUDUSD (forex) with DXY and S&P as correlates / monthly chart only (plus a seasonal chart and a central-bank rate table) / 54 min. About 37 min of slides, then a 17 min worked example.

## Summary
ICT's once-a-month routine for setting a monthly bias. It has nine steps in a fixed order (slide flowchart [chart] ~11:20). The bias and key levels it produces are then carried down to the weekly chart, which is covered in the next lesson. He runs it right after the last trading day of the month closes, and it is meant to cover the next 1 to 3 (up to 4) months. The worked example is a long AUDUSD bias for June 2017, with levels set from the PD array matrix.

## Rules
Order of the process. He calls it time first, then price (~7:27). [new]
1. **Seasonal tendency.** Start from the calendar month you are in or about to begin. Look up the seasonal tendencies that apply. Not every market has one at a given time, so focus on the ones that do (~16:18) [chart]. For equities he names two seasonals a year and prefers the fall seasonal low (~17:41). A seasonal sets the direction he looks in first. It is never forced as a trade (~18:08). [new]
2. **Quarterly shift.** Expect a new 3-4 month leg. Look at the last 9-18 monthly candles to define the long-term trend, ignoring moving averages (~18:43-19:20) [chart]. [new]
   - If the 9-18 month trend is bullish, try to justify why the next quarterly shift is a buying opportunity. Mirror this for bearish. Do not try to pick the end of the trend.
   - If the 9-18 month trend is unclear or consolidating, expect the direction of the previous 3-4 months to reverse, at least as a retracement over the next month or two (~20:16) [chart].
3. **Interest rate differentials.** For FX, pair a high-rate central bank with a low-rate one (investing.com/central-banks) (~20:49) [chart] [forex-only]. For stocks, use bonds: falling bonds mean rising rates, which is bearish for stocks, and rising bonds are supportive of a stock bull market. Commodities work the other way round (~8:10). [new]
   - Ideally seasonal, quarterly shift and rate differential all agree (~21:46).
4. **Market profile.** These are asked in order (~29:26) [chart]: [new]
   - Is it consolidating? Yes: look for evidence of which side breaks out first. No: the trend may be reaching an extreme, so a retracement is likely.
   - Is it trending? Yes: trade continuation and avoid picking the top or bottom. No: look for intermarket signs of the breakout direction.
   - Is it retracing? Yes: use the PD array matrix to find how deep it goes, then trade the continuation. No: go back to the consolidation and trending questions.
5. **Intermarket confirmation.** Look for agreement in positively correlated markets and opposition in negatively correlated ones (bullish dollar should mean weak gold) (~27:17) [chart]. Safe-haven flows (war risk) break the usual correlations (~28:42). [new]
6. **Market structure.** Classify the recent highs and lows. Lower highs after higher highs suggest an intermediate- or long-term high is in control, so look short. Higher lows suggest a long-term low is in control. Look for SMT divergence against the correlated market (~23:19-26:19) [chart]. Favour trades in the direction of current structure. If everything is aligned (structure, SMT, seasonal, rates), never fade it, not even with a scalp (~26:49). [new]
7. **PD array matrix.** Take the portion of price action you have chosen (usually the last 9-18 months) and split it into premium and discount at equilibrium (50%). Mark only the arrays that are obvious. Not every range has every array (~32:22, ~49:40) [chart]. [new]
8. **Key price levels.** Round each PD array to the nearest 0 or 5 level (slide: ".10 level or ._5 level") [chart]. Round premium arrays above price **down**, and discount arrays below price **up**. That is, round toward the current price, never past the array, so the level is easier to reach (~34:00-35:07). [new]
9. **Monthly bias defined.** Carry it over to the weekly chart (~36:00) [chart]. Getting about half of the next monthly candle right is enough to be profitable (~15:37).
- **Timing:** do the analysis once a month, at the close of the last trading day of the month. If the month ends midweek, do it immediately so you are ready for the next day (~1:45, ~11:53). [new]
- **Inflation filter (optional):** most commodities rising means inflation; most falling means deflation (~13:31). [new]
- **Day-trader use:** the monthly bias sets the only direction for intraday trades. He was long-only on AUD scalps through June-July (~52:52). This is consistent with existing HTF-bias usage. [new]
- **Monthly failed break:** after a consolidation, price breaks one side's equal highs (a stop run), then comes back into the range past its midpoint. Expect it to reach the opposite end, or at least the opposite candle bodies, because wicks can be "erroneous" (~45:25-46:05) [chart]. [new]

## Trade examples
- **AUDUSD monthly, bias set at the end of May 2017 for June** (~39:00-52:52) [chart]. The chart is blanked after May.
  - Seasonal: AUD tends to make lows in late January, March and June/July, with weakness from August to October (~37:42) [chart, seasonal chart 1986-2015].
  - Quarterly shift: March to May was three down months. Over 18 candles the market was in consolidation, so a reversal or retracement was expected.
  - Rates: RBA 1.50% against the Fed at 1.00% before the June 14 hike. AUD has the higher yield, which is bullish (~42:00) [chart: investing.com table].
  - Intermarket: DXY had consolidated from about Mar 2015 to Oct 2016 (green box), made a false break above equal highs in Dec 2016, then fell back through the range midpoint. That points to lower dollar prices (~44:20-46:05) [chart].
  - SMT: DXY made a higher high in Dec 2016 compared with Dec 2015. AUD made a higher low (Dec 2016 low 0.7162 against the Jan 2016 low near 0.683) instead of a lower low. This is accumulation in AUD (~46:16-47:24) [chart].
  - Discount arrays found:
    - Bullish order block: the open of the lowest down-close candle, Dec 2016 (O 0.7380 H 0.7525 L 0.7162 C 0.7216, read from the chart tooltip).
    - Rejection block: Dec 2016 close at 0.7216 down to its low.
    - Sell-stop liquidity below the low at 0.7162.
    - No FVG, liquidity void, vacuum gap, breaker or old high was present (~48:49-49:33) [chart].
  - June opened and traded down to exactly 0.7380 (~47:49). No rounding was needed because it is already a 0 level. His rounding example: 0.7382 above price would become 0.7385 (~50:33). Equilibrium of the range was above, so price was in a discount at a discount array.
  - Targets: buy stops above the range high (Mar 2017 high, about 0.775). Next is the rejection block at the Apr 2016 high, about 0.7834/0.7838 line, then the highest up-close candle, about 0.79 (~51:51-52:18) [chart; values approximate].
  - Outcome: June and July rallied to about 0.8065 and went through all targets [chart].
  - Extra confirmation: AUD tracks the S&P, which was rising (~53:44).

## Definitions
- **Quarterly shift:** a new 3-4 month directional leg, either continuation or reversal (~5:40).
- **Bullish order block (monthly):** the open of the lowest down-close candle before the rally that takes it out (~47:42).
- **Rejection block (discount):** from the down candle's close/body to its low, with entry at the close (0.7216) (~48:57) [chart: box drawn from body to low].
- **Market profile:** whether price is trending, consolidating or retracing. This is not volume profile (~8:45).

# cc10_004 ICT Mentorship Core Content - Month 10 - Premium Vs. Carrying Charge Market (WsolkBzpDOQ)
Recorded June 2017 (commodity lesson 4, prices as of 8 June 2017). Shows soybeans, feeder cattle, live cattle and cotton: Barchart contract tables, a daily chart of July 2017 cotton, and a July-October spread chart. 19 min.
**Mostly commodity-specific.** The only transferable idea is a divergence between a market and a related series at a discount PD array (like SMT). The spread tool assumes physical supply and demand, which equity index futures lack.

## Summary
Compare the nearby contract with the next month out. If the nearby is priced lower and later months are progressively higher, it is a carrying charge market (normal). If the nearby is priced higher, it is a premium market: supply is short and commercials want delivery now, which allows fast, parabolic rallies. Plot the spread (nearby minus next month). A bullish divergence (price makes lower lows while the spread rises) at a discount PD array is a buy signal.

## Rules
- Every 2-3 weeks, scan the delivery months for markets building a premium ~1:07 [new] (commodities)
- Premium: nearby price is higher than the next month out. A stronger premium shows when the months beyond keep falling ~4:45-5:20 [chart] [new]
- Carrying charge: each later month is priced higher. Bull moves can still happen, but parabolic moves are less likely ~3:00-3:58 [chart] [new]
- Premium in this sense is not the premium/discount PD-array concept; do not mix the two ~9:44 [new]
- Spread chart: nearby minus next month out, with zero as the key line. The larger the positive spread, the more likely a commercial bull market ~10:31-12:27 [chart] [new]
- Buy signal: in a premium market, price makes lower lows into a discount PD array while the spread rises. Example: an equal-lows sell-stop run into a bullish order block in the discount half of the April range ~13:36-16:29 [chart] [new]
- Exit warning: price makes a higher high with a lower spread peak (bearish divergence). Trail the stop or take partials, then wait for a new buy signal ~17:43-18:37 [new]

## Trade examples
- Cotton, July 2017 contract (CTN17), daily chart, paper/hindsight. In early May 2017, daily lows stepped down into the bullish order block (green box ~76.2-77.0 [chart]). The decline ran below April's equal lows near 77.80 (he says a likely OTE), while the CTN17-CTV17 spread rose. Bought ~77 and it ran to ~87 in under a week: 10 cents, about $5,000 per contract (1 cent = $500) ~16:00-17:06 [chart].
- The spread peaked at ~6.50 at the price high in mid-May. By June it had collapsed to 1.67, and price fell back to ~76 ~12:37 [chart].

## Definitions
- Nearby contract: the first delivery month listed under cash. Next month out: the one after it ~2:30
- Carrying charge market: later months priced progressively higher. Soybeans 8 June 2017: Jul 940-0, Aug 943-4, Sep 943-4, Nov 945-2, Jan'18 952-6, with cash at 905-0 ~3:20 [chart]
- Premium market: nearby priced above later months. Feeder cattle: Aug 154.80, Sep 154.125, Oct 152.775, Nov 151.275, Jan ~145. Live cattle: Jun 131.20, Aug 124.175, Oct 120.40 ~4:05-7:27
- Commercial bull market: commercials pay up for immediate delivery because supply is short ~6:22
- Spread divergence: price and the nearby/next spread disagree at a swing ~13:50

## Seen only on the charts
- The soybean table shows that the curve is not monotone: Jul'18 967-0 and Aug'18 958-0, after which it inverts to ~945 in 2019. He compares only the first few months [chart]
- The overlay chart shows the spread peaking at the same time as the price high (~87), then both falling. In June the spread dropped faster than price, which is a bearish lead [chart]
- The spread line moves in step with price swings from January to May, so it behaves like an oscillator [chart]

## Open questions
- Transfer to ES/NQ: index futures are always in contango (fair value from interest minus dividends), so the front-next spread mostly reflects rates, not demand. Probably not usable; treat as commodity-only unless a later lesson says otherwise.
- He never says how many bars the divergence needs, or how big the spread change must be.

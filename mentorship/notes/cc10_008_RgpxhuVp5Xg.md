Slide lecture, no trades taken. Bond-specific, but he says bond consolidation spreads to all asset classes, so it is a day-type filter for index futures too.

## Summary
How to anticipate a consolidation (small-range) day from the calendar and from price reaching a higher-timeframe PD array or equilibrium, and how to behave on one: small AM scalps only, done before 11:00, PM only if AM has not run stops, limit exits beyond the target. A consolidation day signals that a large-range/trending day follows soon.

## Rules
- Consolidation-day predictors: no noteworthy NY-session reports today, and high/medium-impact US reports due later in the week ~1:51-2:20 [chart] [new]
- If the only high-impact event is a 2pm FOMC, expect consolidation into and through the AM session, with volatility arriving in the PM ~2:56-3:19 [new]
- Formation triggers: price meets a higher-timeframe premium/discount array (profit taking, pause), or reaches the equilibrium (50%) of a higher-timeframe swing; expect a pause, possibly repeated tests, not an instant reaction ~3:24-6:48 [chart] [new]
- US bank holidays and the trading day(s) before them are consolidation days ~6:54-7:27 [chart] [new]
- Bond auctions: the day before can consolidate; the AM session on auction day usually consolidates. Avoid trading the AM on bond auction days ~7:29-7:47, ~11:07 [chart] [new]
- If bonds are in consolidation, expect the same in other asset classes ("stranglehold on volatility") ~8:47, ~18:09 [chart] [new]
- AM scalps on consolidation days: 5-10 ticks (bonds), only if the opening range is small; small = 12 ticks or less in bonds, which tends to squeeze then snap in the order-flow direction, often through an overnight high/low ~9:01-10:13 [chart] [new]
- PM scalps on consolidation days: only if the AM has NOT yet run a stop/liquidity pool; once the PM takes those stops, take profit and stand aside ~10:16-10:57 [chart] [new]
- Avoid the PM session when interest-rate drivers are due (usually 14:00 NY), however good it looks ~11:18-11:28 [chart] (consistent with Ep 03 FOMC rule)
- On expected small-range days do all trading before noon, preferably before 11:00, then stop for the day ~16:59-17:19 [chart] [new]
- When a HTF PD array is hit, switch to scalper mode or sidelines; no big targets ~12:14-12:43 [chart] [new]
- Small ranges precede large ranges on the daily ~12:46 [chart] [new]
- Limit exits should exceed the target (e.g. aim for 8 ticks but rest the limit at 16) and manage the trade live; overshoot pays a bonus ~14:20-15:51 [chart] [new]
- Overnight (London) short-term highs/lows are the low-resistance liquidity runs (LRLR) the NY AM reaches for on consolidation days ~15:59-16:30 [chart] [new]
- After identifying a consolidation day, expect the next day or one soon after to be a long-range/trending day ~17:36-17:52 [chart] [new]
- On expected low-volatility days, cut FX/other expectations to small, low-hanging gains ~24:43-24:55 [chart]

## Definitions
- Consolidation day: small-range, low-volatility day, predicted by calendar (news elsewhere in the week, holidays, auctions) or by price reaching a HTF PD array / equilibrium ~1:05-7:47
- Small opening range (bonds): 12 ticks or less (defined in "lesson one" of the bond series) ~9:27-9:44
- LRLR: low-resistance liquidity run (slide abbreviation) ~16:00 [chart]
- Bond tick = 1/32 = $31.25 per contract ~9:12

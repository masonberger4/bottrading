# cc04_014 ICT Mentorship Core Content - Month 04 - Double Bottom Double Top (owq30ATPU5s)
2016-17 Core Content (teaching 8 of 8, "December 2016" month). Market: AUDUSD (forex), 1h
chart only, examples from Nov 2016 and Jun 2016. Duration ~15 min. Concepts transfer to
index futures; pip figures are forex-only.

## Summary
Double tops/bottoms (relative equal highs/lows) are not support/resistance but liquidity pools
the algorithm will run. ICT maps both sides of liquidity around the double top, then projects
the consolidation range (high to the low between the two peaks) beyond the double top/bottom
to get the "algorithm objective" for the stop run, which then often reverses.

## Rules
- Never trust a double top/bottom as S/R; treat it as buy stops above / sell stops below that
  price will eventually run, even if it takes a long time (~12:02, ~14:04). Consistent with
  "too clean equal highs/lows are magnets" (Ep 04).
- Before acting, map both sides of liquidity: above (the double top, a liquidity void, bearish
  OB) and below (FVG, bullish OB). Price usually takes the nearer/indicated side first, then
  runs the double top (~4:15-6:03) [new as an explicit two-sided map].
- Double-top measured move [new]: measure the range from the double-top high down to the low
  between the two peaks, project that distance above the double top; that is the stop-run
  target (~8:40-10:15) [chart]. Mirror for double bottoms: from the high between the lows down
  to the low, projected below (~10:55-11:13) [chart].
- After the projection is hit, expect a spike reversal back toward the opposite reference
  (double bottom run then rally back to the double-top level) (~11:16-11:59) [chart].
- High-probability trades sit at the extremes of the range, low probability in the middle;
  double tops/bottoms frame those extremes (~12:31-12:47) [new].
- Bullish OB: last down candle before the up move; ICT focuses on its open as the level
  (~3:54-4:04). Bearish OB here = the last two up candles; the liquidity void above the double
  top closed exactly at the open of the candle that began the void (~6:38-7:14) [chart].
- Stop-run sizing [new, forex]: on a 15m chart a stop run is typically 10-20 pips beyond the
  old high/low; on the 1h chart use the range projection instead (~13:04-13:30). Index
  equivalent not given.
- Applies on all timeframes (~14:52).

## Trade examples
- AUDUSD 1h, Nov 2016: relative equal highs at ~0.7700 under a liquidity void up to the open of
  the candle that started the drop (0.7742) [chart]. Below: FVG and bullish OB (~0.7622 level
  marked). Scenario: drop into FVG/bullish OB, then rally through the double top to close the
  void. Outcome: high printed 0.7742 = that candle's open "to the pip" (~6:54-7:04), then
  price reversed and ran sell-side below the lows. No entries/stops/size given.
- AUDUSD 1h, Jun 13 2016: double top ~0.7411; buy from a bullish OB below; projection of the
  range (~0.7378-0.7411) above 0.7411 gives 0.7444/0.7445; high came in 0.7446 (1 pip off),
  then sold off (~9:13-10:24) [chart]. A stochastic shows divergence at the top, shown only
  "for completeness" (~9:31-9:44).
- AUDUSD 1h, Jun 15-16 2016: double bottom ~0.7333; range (~0.7378 to ~0.7333, ~48 pips)
  projected down to 0.7285; low printed at that line, then price rallied back to the 0.7411
  double-top reference (~10:39-11:31, ~13:43) [chart].

## Definitions
- Double top/bottom = two highs/lows in close proximity (relative equal highs/lows) (~2:17).
- Liquidity void = range price ran through with one-sided delivery (only down candles) (~3:18-3:30).
- "Algorithm objective" = projected range beyond the double top/bottom (~11:07).

## Seen only on the charts
- Red boxes: the measured range and its equal-size projection stacked above/below the
  double-top/bottom horizontal line; blue horizontal lines at 0.7411, 0.7444, 0.7285 [chart].
- In the Jun double-bottom example the range box spans from the consolidation high before the
  lows down to the double-bottom low, i.e. the whole consolidation, not just between two lows.

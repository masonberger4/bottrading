# cc05_015 ICT Mentorship Core Content - Month 05 - Trade Conditions & Setup Progressions (Y1oxwc354oo)
Lesson "6.2" of the Jan 2017 mentorship (follow-on to 6.1). Market: USDJPY (forex; JPY cash futures on barchart mentioned). Timeframes: monthly, weekly, daily. Duration ~28 min. Example period: Aug 2016 low to Dec 2016 high (the "1200 pip move").

## Summary
Position-trading lesson: map monthly and weekly PD arrays (premium above equilibrium, discount below) and carry them onto the daily chart. Buy a bullish PD array in a monthly/weekly discount, target the premium arrays in order. Key idea for any timeframe: when a daily PD array fails, price usually drops to the next weekly (then monthly) PD array, which is a re-entry, not a reason to give up the bias.

## Rules
- PD array matrix, ranked from equilibrium outward. Premium (sell / long targets): mitigation block, bearish breaker, liquidity void, FVG, bearish order block, rejection block, old high/low. Discount (buy / short targets): mitigation block, bullish breaker, liquidity void, FVG, bullish OB, rejection block, old low/high. ~1:49-3:41 [chart] [new]
- Long targets are taken in order from nearest: mitigation block first, then breaker (only if a strong force can push through it), then liquidity voids/FVGs, bearish OB, rejection block, old high. ~2:19-2:57 [new]
- From a premium or discount, price first seeks to rebalance to equilibrium (50%) of the most recent range; aiming only for equilibrium is a valid, frequent trade. ~4:03-4:38 [new]
- At equilibrium, expansion can go either way; higher-TF market structure decides the side, never pick at random. ~5:09-5:33
- Keep monthly and weekly PD arrays on the chart even as a day trader; they often matter intraday. ~19:49-20:10 [new]
- PD array hierarchy on failure: if a daily bullish PD array does not hold, expect the next weekly bullish PD array; if that fails, the monthly one. A stop-out from a daily level that ends at a weekly OB is a re-buy, not a bias change. ~18:45-19:31, ~25:22-27:33 [new]
- If a sudden surge is confusing on a lower TF, check the weekly (then monthly) chart for the array being reached. ~26:27-26:40, ~27:24-27:33
- The algorithm works mainly on daily levels; when those have been traded to repeatedly (liquidity absorbed), it reaches for weekly levels, which tend to give large moves. ~27:35-28:13
- In an uptrend focus on down-close candles: institutions buy at their creation and add when price returns to them. ~16:25-16:43 (consistent with Ep 07's "buy down candles")
- When price is near a higher-TF premium target that is still unmet, a down-close daily candle is the new buying opportunity. ~24:46-25:01

## Definitions
- Rejection block: price runs through the wicks below (or above) a group of candles with roughly equal bodies, takes the stops, and rejects back inside the bodies. ~9:50-10:10, ~13:31-13:46 [new] (fills the gap noted in Ep 03)
- Order block of several consecutive down candles: blend the bodies; the OB starts at the highest open in the series (lowest-risk alternative: the last candle's open for a tight stop). ~8:38-8:53, ~16:46-17:11 [new]
- Breaker (bearish): old high, rally clears the buy stops above it, then price trades down through the last down candle; once that candle is violated, it is a valid breaker. Its body midpoint, extended right, is the level to watch. ~10:17-10:49 [refines Ep 05]
- Not an FVG: a range price has already traded through twice (e.g. the OB candle's own wick) is "closed in"; then use the OB's open rather than its wick. ~11:29-12:00 [new]
- Discount level: a level price moved sharply away from, leaving a one-sided gap. ~9:05-9:27

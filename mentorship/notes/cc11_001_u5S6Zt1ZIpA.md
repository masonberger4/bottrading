## Summary
A "mega-trade" (term taken from Larry Williams) is a large yearly price swing with institutional sponsorship plus real supply/demand drivers. Process: check headlines, prefer markets whose nearby contract trades at a premium to distant months, confirm with the dollar index (inverse), pick the relative-strength leader in each sector, diversify across sectors with heavier weight on the strongest leaders. Little here applies to intraday index futures beyond the SMT/relative-strength idea.

## Rules
- Relative strength inside a sector (bullish): the market that refuses to make a lower low while its peers do is the one under accumulation; buy that one. Bearish mirror: the one that fails to make a higher high (~19:50-21:18, ~25:45) [chart]. Same idea as our ES-vs-NQ SMT pick in Ep 03; not new for the bot.
- Second filter among sector leaders: prefer the one that breaks short-term highs / bearish order blocks first and holds at down-close candles (bullish OBs) (~28:37-29:35). [new] as a ranking rule; for index futures this would mean preferring the instrument (ES vs NQ) that breaks its short-term highs first. Our reading.
- Dollar index: generally dollar down = commodities up, dollar up = commodities down, but they can move together for up to a year (~15:02-15:34). Commodity-only filter.
- Nearby contract trading above distant contracts (backwardation, "premium") favours fast parabolic bull moves; not required, just raises odds (~12:15-14:32) [chart]. Commodity-only.
- Sentiment extremes in headlines (a "hot commodity" story after a long move) are contrarian signals, best when the technicals agree (~11:17-11:45).
- Allocation: one leader per sector, but put more capital (example ~50%) into the strongest 2-3 leaders (~30:08-31:07). Long-term portfolio idea only.
- Avoid thin markets (oats, rice, canola, palladium, platinum) because of limit moves (~5:04, ~7:39).

## Definitions
- Mega-trade: large annual price swing that outperforms related markets, institutionally sponsored, supply/demand driven (~1:11).
- Premium (commodities): nearby delivery month priced above distant months; normal (carrying-charge) markets have distant months higher (~12:43) [chart: ZS soybeans contract list, Jul 7 2017; that list shows a normal carrying-charge structure, no premium].
- Commercial bull market (with premium) = parabolic and fast; carrying-charge bull market = stair-step and slow (~14:32).

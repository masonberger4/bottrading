## Summary
Second of the Month 12 capstone lessons: "weekly to daily" (intermediate-term) top-down analysis.
ICT gives a fixed 8-step checklist that ends in a weekly bias and a set of weekly PD-array levels, which
are then carried onto the daily chart. He runs it on the continuing AUDUSD June 2017 example
(monthly bullish order block -> weekly refinement -> rally through the monthly rejection block and old high).

## Rules
The weekly process, in this order (slides ~2:42-8:22, details ~8:22-20:30) [chart]:
1. Relative strength (start here, especially if the monthly picture is unclear). Leaders in strength fail to
   make lower lows; leaders in weakness fail to make higher highs. Want the strongest for longs and the weakest
   for shorts (~8:24-9:41) [chart] [new]. For index futures this is the same idea as the ES/NQ SMT comparison
   already in the rules.
2. Commitment of Traders: commercials at a 6- or 12-month extreme in net holdings (also screen 2- and 4-year
   extremes); trade with large funds in the middle of the range, commercials mark the extremes (~9:44-11:48) [chart] [new].
3. Market sentiment: fade the "big story" in headlines (IBD, Barron's, WSJ, Bloomberg) and retail forums; plus a
   weekly Williams %R (periods 20, 14 or 10; pick the one that best lines up with past important highs/lows)
   (~12:17-14:19) [chart] [new]. All three together form the sentiment opinion.
4. Market profile, three questions (~14:24-15:47) [chart]:
   - Consolidating? yes: expect expansion to show evidence before the breakout; no: trend may be at an extreme, retracement likely.
   - Trending? yes: continuation trades only, no top/bottom picking; no: look for signs of a directional breakout (use intermarket).
   - Retracing? yes: look for continuation after the retracement; no: decide consolidation vs trending with the above.
   He prefers a retracement over a reversal as the first expectation (~14:55) [new].
5. Intermarket / correlation: bullish structure in your market should be supported by positively correlated
   markets and opposed by negatively correlated ones (bullish GBP = weak USD) (~15:57-16:30) [chart]. Includes
   USDX SMT and correlated-pair SMT, and dollar-vs-commodities (~17:16, ~19:55).
6. Market structure with institutional order flow: in bullish structure down-close candles (discount arrays)
   support price and up-close candles (premium arrays) get broken; mirror for bearish (~6:38-7:04, ~16:32-16:58).
   Classify each recent high/low as long-, intermediate- or short-term and decide which is controlling price;
   favor trades in the direction of current structure AND the higher-TF (monthly) bias (~17:25-17:48) [chart] [new].
7. PD array matrix: pick the price range to work in, split into premium/discount, note only the obvious arrays
   on both sides; arrays missing on the higher TF may appear on this lower TF (~7:21-7:45, ~17:53-18:25) [chart].
8. Key price levels: round each PD array to the nearest .10 or ._5 level; premium arrays above price round DOWN,
   discount arrays below price round UP ("calibration") (~18:29-18:58) [chart] [new]. Then state the weekly bias
   and transpose all levels to the daily (~19:02-20:30) [chart].

Carrying levels down:
- Copy every monthly level onto the weekly, then refine: a monthly order block can be narrowed to the weekly
  rebalance point (where a later candle traded back into the gap left above a short-term high) and a weekly
  bullish order block / FVG inside it (~23:19, ~25:24-26:22) [chart] [new].
- Once price runs outside a range (external range liquidity), that range is no longer the dealing range; its
  levels stay as discount arrays below price, and the next range is set by the next premium arrays above
  (~22:23-23:52) [chart].
- Equilibrium (50%) of the weekly swing: price may reach it but should not violate it in a bullish retrace (~26:38) [chart].
- A down-close candle becomes a bullish OB when an up-close candle trades through it; expect a return to it (~27:03-28:09) [chart].
- Time-of-day tools are not used on monthly/weekly; they belong to the daily and below (~46:41) [new].
- Do not apply every concept on every TF; each TF gets its own tools (~46:14) [new].

## Definitions
- ICT hedging program (COT): take the commercials' net position over the last 12 months, mark highest and
  lowest readings, use the midpoint as the zero line; above = buying, below = selling (~11:20-11:48, ~29:12-30:03, ~42:42) [new, forex/futures only].
- Rebalance: a later candle trades back through the range left above a broken short-term high, offsetting
  the one-sided delivery (~25:44-26:10).
- Expansion swing: the leg after a retracement; tends to travel further than the impulse swing (~44:24) [new].

## Open questions
- Steps 1-3 (relative strength across asset classes, COT, headline sentiment) need data the bot lacks; skip or
  use an ES/NQ relative-strength proxy?
- Rounding to ".10 or ._5" is forex pip language; the index-futures analogue (e.g. nearest 5 or 10 ES points)
  is not stated.
- Williams %R period choice is curve-fitted to past highs/lows by eye; no objective rule.
- How "long-, intermediate-, short-term" highs/lows are classified is referred back to earlier months, not restated.

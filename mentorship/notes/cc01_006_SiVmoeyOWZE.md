## Summary
"Fair value" has two meanings: (1) equilibrium, the 50% of a defined high-low range, and (2) the
market maker's view: a price is fair for them to buy in a discount and to sell/scale out in a
premium, most often inside a liquidity void / FVG left by a fast one-way move. He blends range
position (thirds, 50%, OTE), most recent structure break, order blocks and voids into a bias and a
weekly objective (AUDUSD 0.7665, hit).

## Rules
- Buy only in a discount: the lower third of the current range / most recent impulse swing; sell in the upper third (premium). Thirds are stricter than the 50% split. [new] ~2:07-2:36
- At equilibrium the market can go either way; take direction from the most recent structure break (swing high broken -> long bias, swing low broken -> short). [new] ~7:21-7:46
- Bullish setup = overlap: (a) price in the lower third of the parent swing, (b) below 50% of the smaller recent range, (c) at a bullish order block (last down candle before the move up that broke a short-term high), (d) open void/FVG plus buy stops above as targets. Do not buy after the swing high is already broken (that is a premium). [new] [chart] ~9:26-11:22, ~19:41-20:59
- A 62% retracement into a level alone is not a reason to fade; Fibonacci without context trips you up. [new] ~19:41-20:07
- Turtle soup check: after buy stops are run, a real reversal should drop quickly; if price instead goes sideways in a consolidation near its midpoint, expect continuation up. Mirror for shorts. [new] ~14:38-15:35
- Price consolidating around a small range's 50% = market makers holding at fair value; the next expansion leaves that range. Anticipate the breakout, do not trade the breakout itself. [new] ~15:06-15:35, ~29:08-29:27
- Targets for longs: the liquidity void / FVG above (it need not fill all the way), buy stops above old highs, and the low of the bearish order block (up candle before the drop). Shorts mirror: voids left by fast rallies, sell stops below lows, lower end of the range. [new] [chart] ~10:33-10:45, ~24:57-25:21
- Scale out through the void, not all at the far edge, because price may only partly fill it and turn. [new] ~24:09-24:30
- Set the exit ~10 pips before the level you expect to be hit (0.7665 vs 0.7675). Consistent with Ep 08 "exit 0.5-1 point before". ~23:13-23:32 [chart]
- Bodies should stay inside/respect the fair-value zone on the return; wicks can tag it to the pip. [chart] ~17:40-17:49
- Market cycle: price moves sell stops -> buy stops and back, and between discount, fair value and premium. [new] ~25:47-26:11
- Forex-only: dollar (DXY) weakness was a factor but not discussed; open interest is said to help only for commodities. ~8:55, ~29:35

## Definitions
- Equilibrium / fair value (range): 50% of a defined high-low range. ~6:14-6:40
- Discount / premium: below / above equilibrium; best buys in the lower third, best sells in the upper third. ~2:07-2:45
- OTE: 62-79% retracement, 70.5% marked. ~12:04-12:17 [chart]
- Liquidity void: a fast, large-range, small-wick, one-direction run where price spent little time; it is expected to be revisited and filled later (maybe much later on higher timeframes). [conflict] mentorship_rules.md (Ep 01) defines a void as a literal gap with no overlap between candles. ~3:13-3:37, ~5:30-6:03
- Fair value gap (this video): the area between the last up candle at the top (its low) and the first up candle at the bottom of the drop (its high), where only down candles traded. He prefers measuring it by the candle bodies. [conflict] Ep 01 uses the 3-candle wick definition. ~3:51-4:57, ~17:14-17:19
- Bullish order block: down candle before a move up; bearish order block: up candle before a move down (labelled "Institutional Selling"). ~9:26, ~20:59-21:14 [chart]
- Market maker fair value: buy at a discount, sell (new shorts or exits) at a premium; not "fair" in the retail sense. ~26:46-27:31

# cc10_002 ICT Mentorship Core Content - Month 10 - Relative Strength Analysis - Accumulation & Distribution (ygtvWKyOzHQ)
Recorded June 2017 (commodity lesson #2). Market: commodity futures basket vs the US Dollar Index (DXY). Timeframe shown: daily charts only, mid-2016 to June 2017 (Barchart). Duration ~40 min. Mostly slides plus side-by-side DXY / commodity daily charts; no intraday trades, no times of day.

## Summary
Relative strength analysis: when DXY is expected to fall, commodities as a group should rally, but only some do so strongly. The leaders are the ones that made a higher low (failed to make a lower low) while DXY made a higher high; that SMT-style divergence marks institutional accumulation. Mirror for shorts. He then walks the whole basket (grains, livestock, foods, fibers, debt, currencies, metals, crude) for the Jan-June 2017 DXY decline and names the leaders. Directly usable for us as an instrument-selection filter (e.g. ES vs NQ vs YM), not as an entry model.

## Rules
- Bias source: DXY sets the tone; DXY up pressures commodities, DXY down allows them to rise. Do the DXY analysis first; a divergence alone is not a trade without the DXY (higher-TF) expectation behind it. ~04:23, ~07:04 [new for commodities; FX correlation already in Ep 06]
- Buy-side selection: among correlated markets, buy the one that fails to make a lower low while the reference (DXY) makes a higher high = "institutionally sponsored rally" / leader. ~05:00-06:30 [chart] [new as a general rule; Ep 03 has the ES vs NQ version]
- Sell-side selection: short the market that fails to make a higher high while DXY makes a lower low (in a DXY-bullish context) = "institutionally sponsored decline". ~12:00-12:56 [chart] [new]
- Compare within a group (grains, meats, currencies, metals): the one member showing the higher low (or lower high for shorts) is the leader; the others that make the new extreme but then move with it are "sympathetic" and should be skipped or deprioritized. ~09:20-11:50, ~13:50-14:30 [chart] [new]
- Signs a leader is under accumulation: short-term highs keep getting broken, declines are shallow, up-close candles/upswings are much larger than down-close ones; premium arrays fail to resist, discount arrays (bullish OBs) hold. Mirror for distribution. ~05:56-06:15, ~36:25-36:41 [chart] [new as a checklist; consistent with Ep 01 "clean delivery"]
- Best entries: leader + context + discount PD array, e.g. the leader retraces into a prior bullish order block when DXY makes its next intermediate-term high (March 2017). Blend PD-array discount with the higher-TF context. ~20:09-21:31 [chart]
- Sympathetic markets can still reach targets but slower, smaller, more lethargic. ~11:20-11:50 [new]
- Debt instruments (bonds) range-bound -> he calls markets "fickle", FX hard to trade; interest rates drive everything. ~27:40-28:47 [new, context only]
- Interest-rate differential (carry) was a bonus for the AUD and NZD leaders. ~29:24, ~32:53 [forex-only]

## Definitions
- Relative strength analysis: picking the most important market to follow for the current bias (the one that leads). ~04:03
- Institutionally sponsored rally / underlying price strength: leader that fails to trade lower (divergence vs DXY), with broken short-term highs and shallow declines. ~04:56 [chart]
- Sympathetic rally / sympathetic price strength: market that makes the lower low with the reference but then rallies in sympathy to the leader. ~10:27 [chart]
- Institutionally sponsored decline / sympathetic decline: mirrors. ~11:57, ~13:03 [chart]
- Failure swing: the higher low (or lower high) that diverges from DXY; he equates it with SMT divergence. ~05:23, ~12:38

## Open questions
- No lookback rule for "higher low vs higher high": which swings (here daily, multi-week) and how simultaneous they must be is left to judgment.
- For our ES/NQ/YM use, the reference market (what plays the role of DXY) is not defined here; Ep 03 compares the indices to each other directly.
- "Short-term highs broken" and "declines shallow" are qualitative; a coder needs thresholds (e.g. down-swing size < X% of up-swing).
- Dollar figures per contract come from slides; ICT does not state entry prices for them.

## Summary
Defines external range liquidity (ERL: stops beyond the current range high/low) and internal range
liquidity (IRL: voids, FVGs and order blocks inside the range). ICT's model: enter on IRL (a
retracement into an order block inside the range), exit on ERL (the stops above the range high).
Trades in line with the monthly/weekly draw are "low resistance liquidity runs"; trades against it
are "high resistance" and should be avoided.

## Rules
- Every trading range has buy-side liquidity above its high and sell-side below its low; liquidity runs exist to pair orders with those resting stops (liquidity pools) ~00:00:50 [chart] [new]
- When the range is likely to hold, liquidity voids and FVGs inside it get filled (gap risk). Being long with a big gap underneath is a risk: price often reprices fast to close it and stops you ~00:01:40 [new]
- Order blocks inside the range get "repopulated" with new orders; market maker buy/sell models form inside ranges ~00:02:30 [new]
- After each new swing, redefine the range as the latest swing high and swing low. A run of the low is external range liquidity relative to that range, even if it is internal to a larger range ~00:05:39, ~00:09:51 [new]
- Core model: enter at internal range liquidity (bearish OB = last up candle for shorts; bullish OB = last down candle for longs), exit at external range liquidity (stops beyond the range low/high) ~00:10:00, ~00:18:58 [new]
- Higher-timeframe bias first: decide on monthly (else weekly) where price is drawn (e.g. an unfilled monthly FVG). Then on daily/4h/1h only take setups in that direction ~00:12:00, ~00:31:10 [new]
- Low resistance liquidity run = a run on short-term highs (bullish HTF) or lows (bearish HTF) in line with the HTF draw; such highs break with little hesitation. High resistance run = trading against the HTF draw: slow, drawn-down or quick reversal trades. Take only the former ~00:12:53, ~00:32:12 [new]
- While HTF is bullish, a decline below a short-term low is a buying opportunity (sell stops taken to accumulate longs), not a reason to avoid the market. Wait for a quick rejection of the low, then a bullish OB (the last down candle before the move up) ~00:24:03-00:26:06 [new]
- Two trader styles: turtle soup (buy a sell-stop raid / sell a buy-stop raid) or "return to fair value" (buy IRL inside the range). Pick one ~00:22:00 [new]
- Minimum swing: skip setups where the distance from the OB entry to the range high (the target) is small. In FX: ~20 pips no, 40+ pips yes on 15m/1h; aim for 50+ (75-80 ideal) so the goal is reachable without the range breaking ~00:33:14-00:36:15 [new] (forex sizing; the index-futures equivalent is not given)
- Choose the timeframe for the target size: bigger objectives need bigger timeframes (100 pips/week means 1h-4h charts); do not take every setup on every timeframe ~00:37:15, ~00:40:15 [new]
- One good setup per week is the goal ~00:22:00 (matches Ep 03)
- Don't call tops. After reaching the HTF target, ask whether price wants a consolidation before more upside ~00:42:15
- He stayed out for the US election (first two weeks of Nov 2016): standing aside around major event uncertainty is fine ~00:23:03, ~00:27:07 [new]

## Definitions
- External range liquidity: buy stops above the current range high / sell stops below the range low ~00:00:50 [new]
- Internal range liquidity: liquidity voids, FVGs and order blocks inside the current range ~00:01:36 [new]
- Gap risk: the market quickly repricing through a level where there was little or no trading (often a single long candle) ~00:01:58
- Low / high resistance liquidity run: see Rules ~00:12:53 [new]
- Bullish OB = last down candle before the up move; bearish OB = last up candle ~00:10:00, ~00:15:54
- He also references an "up candle" with a wick as a reaction level: its open and the next candle's open/wick bound a pass-through range price should revisit ~00:04:38 (unclear)

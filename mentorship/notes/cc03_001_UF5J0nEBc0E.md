# cc03_001 ICT Mentorship Core Content - Month 03 - Timeframe Selection & Defining Setups (UF5J0nEBc0E)
2016-17 Private Mentorship, Month 03, teaching 1 of 8 (slides dated November 2016). Market: forex only (EURUSD monthly/weekly/daily, roughly 2008-2015 swing); no index futures, no intraday charts. Timeframes: monthly, weekly, daily. Duration ~49 min. About 20 min of slides, then one top-down worked example.

## Summary
Matches each chart timeframe to a trading style, and lists five trader "models". Then one EURUSD example: a monthly bearish order block and monthly equal lows define the range, the weekly refines it, and the daily range is graded into quarters where new sell setups form. He ends by saying he trades only three setups: order blocks, stop runs (turtle soup) and liquidity voids / returns into a range.

## Rules
- Timeframe to style: monthly = position trading, weekly = swing, daily = short-term, 4h or less = day trading ~0:54-7:04 [chart] [new]
- Five models (slide) ~13:12-17:31 [chart] [new]:
  1. Trend trader: trades only in the direction of the monthly and weekly charts.
  2. Swing trader: daily-chart intermediate-term price action.
  3. Contrarian: reversal patterns at market extremes (capitulation, or a run above a prior month's high).
  4. Short-term trader: trades the weekly range, held 1-5 days.
  5. Day trader: intraday swings, exits by 2:00 pm New York time. [conflict] mentorship_rules.md trades a PM session from 14:00-16:00 (2023 material). For the AM model, a 14:00 flat cutoff is a reasonable default.
- Use the daily chart as the base reference even if you day trade. It sets the directional bias and shows the "interbank" levels: stops, liquidity voids, FVGs, liquidity above and below ~4:24-6:00, ~19:36-20:11 [new]
- Higher-TF levels set the direction for the lower-TF trades. Your setup pattern is the same on every timeframe; only the waiting time changes ~9:10, ~12:44 [new]
- Trading in the direction of the most recent higher-TF setup gives the lowest-risk, highest-reward trades. You can miss the original entry and still trade later setups in that direction ~21:17-21:56 [chart] [new]
- Locating a bearish order block: find where price moved strongly away from a level (a range break with magnitude), then mark the last up candle before the drop. Its low is the reference to sell on return ~27:31-28:36 [chart] (consistent with Ep 02 OB definition)
- The range for the trade runs from the higher-TF entry PD array (bearish OB) to the opposing liquidity pool (equal lows = sell stops of long-term funds). It is defined before price moves ~34:56-35:26, ~37:49 [chart] [new]
- Grade the swing: lay a fib from the known high to the known low and mark 25 / 50 / 75%. New setups form near those grades and at equilibrium (50%) ~38:04-39:05 [chart] [new]
- Hold the higher-TF bias until price clearly proves it wrong (strong opposite move) or the target (the "terminus") is hit ~42:42-43:02 [new]
- In a higher-TF bearish leg, short-term highs that price takes out "by a little" then drop fast are the sell entries (turtle soup). Without the higher-TF reason, a stop run alone is not a trade ~40:50-42:42 [chart] (Ep 08 turtle soup plan)
- Alternatives within the same leg: sell a return to a breaker (the down candle before the move up through a short-term high) ~43:49-44:46 [chart]; OTE: fib from a swing high to a swing low, short around the 79% retrace ~44:36 [new]; sell a return to a bearish OB ~44:46.
- His three setups ~45:15-46:03 [chart] [new]:
  (a) In a range / liquidity void: after expansion, wait for price to retrace back into the void, then buy (bullish) for a run to the previous high; he says the target is only that known range ~47:52-48:14.
  (b) Bearish: sell a run above a previous high (buy stops).
  (c) Bearish: sell a return to a bearish OB, used when a run above the high is not needed.
  He says choosing (b) vs (c) is taught later; it is not resolved here ~46:03-46:24.
- One good pattern is enough. Pick the one you see most clearly ~45:15, ~46:44-47:43 [new]

## Definitions
- Contrarian trading: reversal patterns at extremes. Capitulation is the last fast burst that usually makes the extreme of the move ~15:46-16:52.
- Swing trade (his definition): one or two trades per quarter, found on the weekly chart ~2:58.
- Optimal trade entry: a return into a known range, measured with a fib. The only "indicator-based" tool he uses ~39:35-39:59.
- Turtle soup = stop run = false breakout of an old high or low ~39:59.
- Breaker (bearish usage here): the down candle right before an up move that takes out a previous high. Price returning to it is a sell ~43:49-44:24 (Ep 01 defines the bullish shape).
- Large-fund liquidity: long-term trend-following funds leave stops below major lows for a long time ~31:45-32:16.

## Seen only on the charts
- The monthly OB line is drawn at the up candle's low (body and wick bottom), not its open ~28:00.

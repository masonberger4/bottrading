## Summary
A framework lecture, not a setup lecture. A "high reward" setup is one where three perspectives agree on direction: big picture (2 of 4 factors), intermediate (2 of 3 factors) and short term (one from each of 3 factors), so "seven things" in agreement. Entry signals come after this filter and are explicitly not covered. The rest is process/discipline talk: binary, flow-chart decisions.

## Rules
- High reward setup = big picture, intermediate and short-term perspectives all point the same direction; trade only that side (~27:28-27:50, ~44:47-45:11). [new]
- Big picture: needs at least 2 of 4 in agreement: macro (inflation/deflation), interest rates (higher/lower/unexpected change, rate differentials), intermarket (CRB commodities vs USDX, usually inverse), seasonal tendencies (~22:40-23:30, ~25:43-28:48) [chart]. [new]
- Intermediate: needs at least 2 of 3: top-down analysis (monthly, weekly or daily; only one of those timeframes is required), COT (commercial hedging, 12-month / 4-year extremes), market sentiment (extremes; least significant) (~29:41-38:30) [chart]. [new]
- Example that is "enough": weekly chart indicates higher prices + COT commercials bullish -> wait for a short-term perspective that lines up with buying (~38:14-38:33). [new]
- Short term: needs one item from each of 3: correlation (USDX SMT / correlated-pair SMT), time & price theory (quarterly, monthly, weekly range, daily range, time of day), IPDA (institutional order flow, liquidity seeking, market efficiency paradigm) (~43:48-44:22) [chart]. [new]
- Total for high reward = 2 + 2 + 3 = "seven things" in agreement (~53:11-54:21). [new]
- This is a filter, not an entry: still wait for an entry signal (~54:23-54:36). [new]
- Cadence: big picture and intermediate set at the start of the week (weekend homework), may change Tue/Wed; short-term perspective changes day to day (~48:38-49:07). [new]
- Time of day alone can make something tradable even when quarterly/monthly/weekly/daily ranges offer nothing (~46:47-47:03). Consistent with existing kill-zone/session rules.
- Correlated pairs out of sync (no symmetry) -> be very selective with trades (~40:45-40:56). Consistent with SMT use in mentorship_rules.md (ES vs NQ / MES).
- Losses mostly come from the short-term layer: forcing trades or the market being in transition; trading with the higher-TF view is an advantage (~52:03-52:37).
- Intraday charts only reflect what monthly/weekly/daily drive; funds analyse monthly/weekly and execute on daily (~32:55-33:48). [new] (supports higher-TF draw-on-liquidity first)
- Decisions must be binary/flow-chart: take action, wait, or go back a stage (~7:42-7:54, ~59:37-59:48).
- Scalping can work using only the short-term list if price action is understood well (~49:41-50:11).

## Definitions
- Quarterly effect: roughly every 3 (3-4) months a new higher-TF price shift; after a trend, expect consolidation or reversal over the next quarter; not tied to calendar month boundaries (~41:04-42:10). [new]
- Monthly effect: reference points from monthly ranges (~42:12). Weekly range: ICT calls himself a weekly-range trader aiming for the "lion's portion" of the week's range ("one shot one kill") (~42:21-42:47). Daily range: power of three / OHLC engineering of the daily candle (~42:48-43:02).
- USDX SMT / "cracking correlation": dollar makes a higher high but e.g. GBPUSD fails to make a lower low (~39:45-40:07). Forex-only form; index analogue = ES/NQ divergence. 
- Symmetrical market: correlated pairs move in tandem; asymmetrical = they don't (~40:28-40:51).
- IPDA = interbank price delivery algorithm; market efficiency paradigm: smart money seeks existing orders (stops above old highs / below old lows) as forced counterparty liquidity, not "their own" supply/demand zones (~47:16-47:52).
- COT: commercials at 12-month extreme vs the net zero line can signal a hedging change and a long-term high/low (~35:14-35:48). Small specs ignored.
- Sentiment: averaged readings (Market Vane, barchart community opinion) checked Saturdays (~30:36-31:26, ~35:58-36:16).

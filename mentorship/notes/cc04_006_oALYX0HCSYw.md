# cc04_006 ICT Mentorship Core Content - Month 04 - ICT Rejection Block (oALYX0HCSYw)
2016-17 Private Mentorship, December 2016, "ICT Teaching 3.3 of 8" (slide title "Reinforcing Orderblock Theory [Rejection Block]"). Market: forex (a daily chart first, then generic candle slides; instrument and prices not legible). Duration ~15 min. Frames viewed: 7.

## Summary
Defines the rejection block, a distribution or accumulation pattern at a swing high or low with long wicks. The block runs from the extreme body price (highest open/close for a high) to the extreme wick. Price only has to trade above the highest body, not make a higher high, for a failure swing. Treat the block like an order block, with its body edge as the trigger. Also use it as a take-profit level.

## Rules
- Bearish rejection block: a swing high formed by one or more candles with long upper wicks. Price trades above the candle bodies to run buy-side liquidity, then declines ~5:04-5:38 [chart] [new]
- Frame it: top = the highest wick of the swing high; bottom (trigger) = the highest open OR close of any candle in that swing. Candle color does not matter ~9:30-10:14 [chart] [new]
- Treat the block like a bearish order block. Trigger = the block's low, which is the highest body price ~10:21-10:43 [chart] [new]
- Entry option 1 (aggressive): sell limit at the trigger, with a "significant" stop above the block (above the highest wick) ~10:43-10:53 [new]
- Entry option 2: let price trade above the trigger by some amount without making a new wick high. Then sell on a sell-stop below the trigger, which sells on weakness. He calls this one of the few times he uses stop-entry orders ~10:58-11:33 [new]
- No higher high is needed for a failure swing: a run above the highest body is enough, which differs from turtle soup ~7:29-7:40, ~14:01-14:14 [new]
- Bullish rejection block: a swing low with long lower wicks; bottom = the lowest wick, top/trigger = the lowest open or close in the swing low. Buy at or just below the trigger, or wait for a trade through it and buy on a buy-stop just above ~12:16-13:41 [chart] [new]
- Ideal setups: bearish in major-to-intermediate downtrends, bullish in uptrends (slide) ~5:11, ~12:16 [chart] [new]
- Targets: rejection blocks are take-profit levels. When short into an old swing low, cover just below that low's lowest open/close; do not require price to reach the wicks ~14:14-14:38 [new]
- Bodies (opens/closes) are the institutional reference; wicks only draw attention to the pattern ~7:51-8:02, ~9:25-9:30, ~14:38-14:44 [new]
- Context: best when the high is into a higher-TF resistance (bearish OB, old high). He warns against reading it as a bull flag ~6:11-6:24

## Trade examples
- Generic candle slide ~11:40-12:10 [chart]: the swing high has one long wick, and its highest body price is the candle's open. A later candle trades a little above that open, makes no new wick high, and price then runs to sell-side liquidity below. No prices.
- Bullish slide ~13:50 [chart]: a horizontal line at the lowest body of the first swing low. The second low wicks just below that line (well above the first low's wick), then a strong rally to new highs.
- Opening daily chart ~1:00-3:20: two "major" turtle soups (false break above an old high, below an old low). Instrument and dates not legible.

## Definitions
- Rejection block (bearish) = the range from the highest open/close to the highest wick of a wicked swing high; bullish = from the lowest open/close to the lowest wick of a wicked swing low ~9:38-10:14, ~12:47-13:01 [chart] [new]
- Turtle soup: false break of an old high/low that runs stops, then a reversal. Previously in the rules; restated ~1:08-1:18, ~2:43-2:49

## Seen only on the charts
- Slide at ~8:20: the "attacking" candle wicks above the prior highest body but below the prior highest wick, a lower wick high. Then a wide-range down candle follows.
- The block diagram draws the block as a standalone candle: top = wick high, bottom = trigger at the body top.

## Open questions
- What counts as a "long wick"? No ratio is given. A coder needs a threshold, e.g. upper wick >= body, or >= X% of the range.
- How many candles form "the swing"? (Fractal swing of N bars? all candles whose highs are within the wick zone?)
- How far past the trigger before the stop-entry is valid ("a significant amount")? Not quantified.
- Is the entry in option 2 placed below the trigger, or below the low of the probing candle? He says "on a stop below that level", so presumably the trigger.

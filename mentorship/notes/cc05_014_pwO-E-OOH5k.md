## Summary
Defines the PD (premium/discount) array matrix: take the current dealing range (recent old high
to old low) on the monthly, weekly or daily chart, split it at equilibrium (50%), and list
premium arrays above and discount arrays below in a fixed order. Price moving away from
equilibrium meets them nearest-first; breakers take precedence and usually stop price from
reaching the arrays beyond them. The same hierarchy applies on every HTF chart.

## Rules
- Frame the dealing range as the most recent swing from an old high to an old low (or reverse) on the HTF chart; the halfway point is equilibrium; above = premium, below = discount ~17:34-18:20 [chart] [new]
- Price moves from discount to premium and back; buy in discount, sell in premium ~9:12-9:50 [chart] (consistent with Ep 03/08 premium/discount use)
- Premium array list, from equilibrium upward (order price meets them): mitigation block, bearish breaker, liquidity void, fair value gap, bearish order block, rejection block, old high/low ~18:20-19:18, ~24:57-25:30 [chart] [new]
- Discount array list, from equilibrium downward: mitigation block, bullish breaker, liquidity void, FVG, bullish order block, rejection block, old low/high ~26:49-29:36 [chart] [new]
- Higher on the premium list = deeper premium; old high/low is the most extreme ~24:57-25:16 [chart] [new]
- When looking for a level to sell into from equilibrium: check the left side for the nearest mitigation block, then bearish breaker, then void, then FVG, then bearish OB (almost always present); if no clear OB (many wicks), use the rejection block, then the old high ~19:34-23:03 [new]
- Breaker precedence: if a breaker sits between price and a void/FVG/OB, expect price not to pass the breaker; the void/FVG beyond it may stay open, and the OB is unlikely to be hit ~21:04-21:51, ~25:48-26:49, ~31:37 [new]
- Without a breaker, expect the void and gap to be filled on the way to the order block ~28:47-29:02 [new]
- An old LOW can act as premium/resistance when price has rallied a long way up into it (and an old high as discount support from far above) ~23:22-23:57, ~29:19 [chart] [new]
- Same hierarchy on monthly, weekly and daily; in this lesson "lower timeframe" means daily, nothing below it ~6:19-6:33, ~30:22-31:37 [chart] [new]
- HTF levels inside a big swing may only be visible on lower charts (a monthly low may hold at a daily short-term high) ~6:04-6:48 [new]
- Preferred pattern at resistance: a second pass toward the old high (run-through, false break or failure swing) rather than a single spike ~4:41-5:15 [chart]
- Market makes no straight line between current and forecast price; known PD arrays on the way help hold the trade ~14:36-14:49 [chart]

## Definitions
- PD array: premium/discount array, the ordered list of institutional reference points ~10:07 [new]
- Equilibrium: midpoint of the current trading range (old high to old low) ~18:04 [chart]
- Buying imbalance = price above equilibrium; selling imbalance = price below equilibrium ~9:30-9:50 [new]
- Rejection block: the area beyond the candle bodies but inside long wicks at a high (bearish: just above the bodies) or low (bullish: just below the lowest body); expected to be run ~18:38-18:54, ~29:02-29:19 [new] (resolves the [corrected] gap in Ep 03)
- Mitigation block: a failure-swing low in an up-move (price failed to make another pass at the old high); longs there are underwater and sell to get out, so price returns to it to sell. Breakers are a type of mitigation block, but mitigation blocks generally sit closer to equilibrium ~7:33-8:20, ~20:50-21:04 [new]
- Bearish breaker: the down candle just before the second high that takes out the old high (turtle soup); bullish breaker: the up candle between two swing lows, the later low lower (stop run) ~21:04-21:22, ~27:55-28:11 [conflict] (Ep 01/05 define the breaker by the swing sequence and a body break; here it is a single candle)
- Fair value gap: price delivered on one side only ~28:47
- Time and price: wait for both to agree; time to reach the target is unknowable ~12:48-13:16 [chart]

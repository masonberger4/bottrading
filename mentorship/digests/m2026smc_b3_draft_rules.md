## Part A. Rules by topic (DRAFT)

### A1. Wick consequent encroachment (the batch's main theme)
- DRAFT: **Wick CE sensitivity filter.** For a bullish wick: "the upper half to consequent encroachment. It can touch it. It can't put a body below it because if it puts a body below consequent encroachment that invalidates the bullishness." Not touching CE at all = strength. Bearish mirror: "We don't want any bodies above that." (073, 071, 072, 076, 079) [refines B2 A2/A4; core]
- DRAFT: **Close beyond a wick's CE targets its extreme.** "If I see a wick like that and it closes below [its CE] ... it's more likely they'll come back for that low" (071). Daily: crude 87.76 close below the 88.25 CE (080); gold and EURUSD the same (075, 080). Failure to close below = "warning sign it's going to change directions" (072). [refines B2 A2]
- DRAFT: **Wick CE as stop boundary.** "If there isn't any inefficiency below ... the next PD array is this wick. So, its consequent encroachment is a barrier or boundary"; stop at the wick low or wick CE (073, 079). [refines B2 A7 stops]
- DRAFT: **Wicks take precedence over the OB.** A large wick beside the largest down-close candle governs: "whenever you have that, just like a gap, wicks take precedence ... Because they're balanced price ranges" (074). [new; conflict check: 2023 OB priority]
- DRAFT: **Discount vs premium wick** depends on where market price sits relative to it (074, 075). London 03:00 traded up into 50% of the previous day's wick and made the daily high (075). [refines B2 A2]
- DRAFT: **Mohawk** = a small wick outside an FVG/IFVG while bodies stay inside; permissible (079, 080). When bearish, "I demand that that second one be lower than the previous one" (085). [new]

### A2. Inversion FVGs and picking the "real" gap
- DRAFT: **IFVG needs a close; OB does not.** "Order block does not require close. Inversion fair value gaps require the close ... put that in your notes" (082). Full validation: "trades above it, fails to leave a body up [there] and closes down below it. That validates this as an inversion fair value gap. Anybody else ... you're actually guessing" (078). [refines B1 IFVG; core, backtestable]
- DRAFT: **First utilization decides the inversion.** "What was its first utilization? ... it was utilized as a bullish fair value gap first", so trading lower makes it bearish (085). An FVG formed before the high is taken that never got its first utilization "should operate under the mode of inversion fair value gap" (081). [refines B2 A5 first utilization]
- DRAFT: **Validity rule.** "The fair value gaps are either first presentation, and if they're not first presented, then they are always anchored to a octant or a quadrant. Period." Anchored = touching a graded level (073, 084). For a BISI, "either the low or consequent encroachment must absolutely must be respected" (085). [new; backtestable filter]
- DRAFT: **FPFVG = the gap that displaces through the swing** "within the context and narrative" (081); prefer the more prominent run (073, 09:35 example). [refines B2 A1 FPFVG on gap days]
- DRAFT: **VI to BPR to IFVG.** A VI created by one delivery and later traded through becomes a balanced price range read as an IFVG; "it's more likely to fail" (074). VI with no body = undelivered price; behaves as a SIBI/BISI (077). [refines B1 IFVG]
- DRAFT: **IFVG retest quality.** "Fails to get to consequent encroachment ... Fails to go higher than the previous wick. That's exactly what you want to see" (081). Retest bodies must not close in the far half; wick only (071). [refines B2 A4]
- DRAFT: **Nested FVGs.** IFVG + fresh opposite FVG stacked = "a variant of a breaker" (078); bullish FVG + bullish IFVG = "two qualified areas for discount sensitivity" (079); breaker + IFVG + bearish FVG after a blow-off (085). [new; conflict check: 2023 breaker]
- DRAFT: **Footholds.** Each IFVG/FVG that holds is a foothold to trail under (087). [new]
- DRAFT: **Stop and reverse.** Failed IFVG short: "close the trade, save the stop loss, reverse, and treat this as the initial utilization, which is bullish" (071); octant short for nearby stops, then long from the reclaimed FVG (076). [new; conflict check: B1 IFVG first presentation vs utilization]

### A3. Order blocks and change in state of delivery
- DRAFT: **CISD entry.** The OB is the opening price of the last opposite candle (in a series of down-close candles, the one with the largest body). "Once it trades above, it does not need to close above it." Entry on the retrace to that exact price: "That's not a demand zone ... It's a very specific price." Premise: discount-to-premium run after a HTF PDA (073). [refines B2 A5 OB; conflict check: 2023 CISD close requirement]
- DRAFT: **OB re-entries.** "Whenever there's an order block that is being implemented and it starts to sell off, and then it goes right back to it again, it's allowing smart money to accumulate more" (072). Bearish OB fills in the lower half (072). [conflict check: 2023 mean threshold]
- DRAFT: **Long frame from a CISD wick:** "anywhere from [the candle close] to the consequent encroachment," stop at the wick low (073). [new]

### A4. Grading, projections and event horizon
- DRAFT: **Unrealized dealing range and the 1.25 projection.** On the day the displacement starts, take the day's opening price to its low, project 1.25 = low-hanging fruit; repeat from the prior close (VI edge, i.e. the body) = extreme; the CE of the two projections was the DXY high to the tick (074). [new; directly backtestable]
- DRAFT: **Fulcrum measured move with standard deviations.** After a "grand finale" blow-off, use the run before it; the broken "interest" level is the fulcrum; -1 and -2 SD of that run (the -2 SD hit 29,878.75 vs low 29,877.5) (085). Measure wicks and bodies, and blend. [new; conflict check: 2023 SD projections]
- DRAFT: **Grade between two same-side pools.** "This low and this low is specifically anchored to its lows ... using two pools of liquidity to grade that range"; sell its premium half; breaking its CE = the signal (084). [new]
- DRAFT: **Event horizon.** Definition restated: only the 0.5 between two pools; "a very, very strong candidate for price reaching to"; used for "predetermined partials and ... weighing the magnitude and velocity." Closing beyond it "gives confidence that we're likely to draw to this level" (074, 075). Nested event horizons = a zone; closing beyond both raises confidence (075). Partials "just below" or "just above" it (071, 072, 076). [refines B1/B2 event horizon]
- DRAFT: **Suspension block grading.** Fade the daily suspension block lower quadrant and octants inside a macro (073); suspension block treated as an IFVG; bodies in its middle then failing CE = weak (074). [refines B1/B2 suspension block]
- DRAFT: **Shadow** = overlap of the daily bearish FVG and the suspension block; grade the overlap too (073). Sixteenths: "split ... in half, you might want to start doing that" (073). [refines B2 shadow; sixteenths new research]

### A5. Daily framing and targets
- DRAFT: **Previous-day range rule.** "When the market is indeed bearish, the market's going to be only retracing in the lower half of the previous day's range. If it does retrace up to half ... most likely it's going to be an afternoon or late session continuation or the next day." Not reaching half = "really bearish" (075). [new; conflict check: 2023 daily range rules]
- DRAFT: **Daily VI as a magnet.** After a big down day, the intraday retrace target is the low/CE of the daily VI it left (079); a gap-day VI whose open = low shows "zero" willingness to go lower and points to the all-time high (085). [new]
- DRAFT: **Gap fill threshold:** on a gap lower, the first target is the previous session's low; the close is the best-case fill (085). [conflict check: 2023 ORG/NDOG]
- DRAFT: **Nearest draw first.** "Whatever one has the least amount of range to get to ... a low-hanging fruit objective" for short-term or low-conviction trades (077). [new; conflict check: 2023 draw selection]
- DRAFT: **Sync intraday with the daily target.** "Every day until those targets on the daily chart are met, strive for entries that put you in sync with that run" (075); HTF analysis "monthly, weekly and daily ... really the daily" (077). [refines B2 A2]
- DRAFT: **Midweek reversal.** A sustained run with one down-close candle near its target on a Wednesday = "we could see a midweek retracement or reversal"; take profit (074). [conflict check: 2023 weekly profiles]
- DRAFT: **Three highs then a Friday washout** = capitulation/Judas on the daily (080). [new]
- DRAFT: **Climax reversal = "the flavor of 2026."** Strong daily close, overnight/London up, then a 07:00-09:00 parabolic run above highs that stops out shorts = "the best kind of scenario ... to short on"; short the first IFVG; one-directional move to the first "interest" level = continuation (085). Beginners (<1-2 years) only go long in a primary bull market. [new; backtestable]
- DRAFT: **Sell stop after a refused draw.** If price fails to reach a known draw, place a sell stop below the last low; the break should be "very, very, very aggressive" (075). [new; backtestable]

### A6. Time windows, macros and day filters
- DRAFT: **Pre-market.** "Between 5:00 a.m. and 6:00 a.m." is dead time; "6:00 a.m. is the earliest that the ... higher probability price runs can be trusted," then 07:00-09:00; especially on NFP weeks ("early bird") (082). 05:50 macro used (082). [new; conflict check: 2023 kill zones]
- DRAFT: **AM macro 09:50-10:10** for shorts at HTF arrays (073, 084, 075). Wait for 10:00 news before entering (071). [refines B1 A6]
- DRAFT: **Lunch.** Lows made at the start of the 09:50-10:10 macro (and 10:01) are the lunch-macro draw; lunch 11:30-13:30, setup in the first hour, hold through 12:30-13:30 (081). [refines B2 A6; see conflict 25]
- DRAFT: **PM / last hour.** Macros 14:50-15:10 and 15:15-15:45 ("sweet spot ... find where it's smooth"); last-hour target = smooth relative equal highs holding earlier shorts' stops (087). PM delivery "usually between 2:30 and 3:45 p.m." (085). MOC inversion FVG in the final minutes; "Market on close is very algorithmic" (073). Post-macro run after 13:50-14:10 (073 PM 14:06 OB). [refines B1 A6; see conflict 24]
- DRAFT: **Open volatility.** "There's volatility in the first couple minutes of 9:30 to 9:40 ... can be furious"; enter at the open only with a cushion from an earlier winner (085). Paper fills unreliable at the open (076). [new; execution realism]
- DRAFT: **Holidays.** Monday after a Saturday holiday is "very very trashy" (071, same rule as B2 070); pre-holiday NFP Thursday = "holiday volume ... squirrely" (072). [same as B2 A3]
- DRAFT: **Summer.** "Slow down in your trades. Look for one or two setups for the week. Once you get it, be done" (073); summer tends to be "gnarly" (085). [refines B2 A3 summer]
- DRAFT: **Two-sided = no trade.** "If you can frame it on both sides of the market ... don't trade it" (073); "no man's land" between two IFVGs: "don't touch it" (078); dead center of the 15m range = ugly (071). [refines B2 A1 no bias]
- DRAFT: **Sticky vs low-resistance conditions.** LRLR = runs leaving small inefficiencies with shallow retracements; one inefficiency shared by many candles = "not an environment you want to be a part of"; if a trade turns sticky, de-risk (084). Time to the first pool is the tell: lethargic = bias probably wrong (085). [refines B2 HRLR/LRLR]

### A8. Entries, exits, management
- DRAFT: **Limit entry a tick beyond the CE** of the entry FVG, stop beyond the VI; "Let it come to you. Don't chase it" (072). If missed, use the next valid entry (CISD/OB) (072). [refines B1 entries]
- DRAFT: **Layered exits.** Bulk at the HTF gap low; stagger limits; trail the stop to the gap low to lock runners (079); stop-loss as the exit for the last contract (071). [refines B2 A7]
- DRAFT: **No re-entry on the same leg** after exiting: "There has to be rules" (072, 076). [new]
- DRAFT: **Volatility risk.** "Shorten the depth of risk"; "pack small and play big"; micros for small/prop accounts (078, 085). [new risk switch]

Annotate in your own words inside the chart: session/market/day/week/month characteristics; "generally time-based deliveries ... very specific PDA responses drawing to an obvious liquidity pool above or below based on bias." Do your own annotation first, then watch his video and compare. Training wheels.
- Calling targets in advance: Monday video said buy from the down-close candle bullish order block to the CE of its wick (about the low of the daily CIBI) then to the CE of the CIBI; both delivered Tue and Wed. Daily CIBI low and its midline (CE) = the draw.
- Opening trade down to a prior high (Friday's high) then back up: "when it does that and trades above the open, that's one of the most powerful bullish scenarios when you have a bias leaning higher."
- If price gets outside a CIBI, the next one is the draw; a gap can become a bullish inversion FVG; relative equal highs above are "suspect" (to be taken). "Avoiding trying to pick a top."
- Hourly: buy side imbalance sell side inefficiency (BISI) low; rally, come back down using the high of the BISI; above the high comes **immediate rebalance** (bodies respect); then it gravitates to the daily CIBI low. 15-minute BISI traded up falling short of the midpoint (CE), bodies respecting.
- **Non-farm payroll week:** "Strive to look for setups on Monday of NFP week." Beginners avoid Mondays otherwise. Wednesday morning of NFP week = time distortion, consolidation, building sell liquidity; Wednesday afternoon/Thursday hit-or-miss; "Friday 8:30 the carnival ride unloads." With experience, trade any day; teaches a few setups per week; "day trading is not everyday trading." Pick the most advantageous days, times, days of week and months.

## Rules (what ICT says)
- Use overnight/weekend (electronic trading hours) levels for structure and liquidity, not only regular trading hours (RTH). Know RTH buy side too.
- NWOG: Friday settlement to Sunday 18:00 open. Grade it with quadrants (lowest quadrant, equilibrium = consequent encroachment (CE) because it is a gap, upper quadrant, and the actual opening price). Keep it on the chart for the whole week and for about five weeks (older ones also usable).
- Read price's reaction to the NWOG: bodies/wicks that fail to reach CE or the upper quadrant while accumulating = bullish ("under accumulation"); the dive that runs retail stops (harmonic/Gartley style levels) and then fails to reach the NWOG low = more accumulation; the pre-9:30 displacement down into an inefficiency then rally into RTH buy side is the Monday pattern ("fast bias").
- Principle: PD arrays/gaps that price does NOT trade to while bullish (e.g. never reaching a discount) are strength information; price "has somewhere to be".
- Set an upside objective before the open: for the morning session ICT names the furthest he expects (hourly balance price range, BPR) and says it may go higher but "I'd move without it".
- You do not need to wait for a first presented FVG to trade the opening range if you have mapped where price should go and where the run originates (the FVG is a teaching device; knowing where it goes next and where it originates is the baseline).
- Minimum learning set: what makes it bullish, what makes it bearish, then frame the narrative: where it reaches next and which PD arrays are the "handholds" (rock-climber analogy: map footholds on the left before climbing).
- Candlesticks: use pure OHLC time-based charts; distorted candles (Renko-like gimmicks) hide the algorithm's reference points. No volume, footprint, level 2, harmonics, Fibonacci ratios, Gann, Hurst cycles.
- Balance price range (BPR): any portion of price action with delivery in both directions. Built from a buy-side imbalance sell-side inefficiency (BISI) overlapping a sell-side imbalance buy-side inefficiency (SIBI); "not a suspension block because a suspension block needs volume imbalance both ends". Example: an FVG anchored to an order block (3 down-close candles = one block of sell-side delivery, open = change in state of delivery near the gap midpoint) is preferred over a lone FVG. The BPR is a formidable obstacle (price cuts through only on unexpected news/geopolitics).
- Grade an inefficiency between two PD arrays by taking the high of one to the low of the other and reading the quadrants ("dynamic range"). Answer to when a gap is respected: only to its lowest portion then reverse; to half (CE) then reverse; or fully overlapped then used as a "trampoline" (discount array).
- Opening range gap (ORG): previous settlement (Friday for Monday) to 09:30 open. Roughly 70% chance the midpoint is traded to by about 10:00 ("not so demanding that it does it today"). Keep separate workspaces for NWOG, NDOG, and ORG levels; eventually trade the chart naked, levels on paper.
- First presented FVG: minimum is the 09:31 one-minute candle forming with an FVG, for rule-based simplicity (for his son Caleb). He avoids the extra rules of trading a 09:30 FVG ("Pandora's box"); sticking to 09:31 yields more clarity. Minor buy side / sell side = first range formed inside the first 30 minutes.
- If/then: if price takes out the opening-range minor high and closes above the quadrant, it continues to the BPR; if it takes the high and falters/closes back below the candle close (rejection block), it likely runs the sell side and the ORG upper quadrant/CE becomes primary. While price sits in the middle, do nothing.
- Closing/rejection test: after trading above a level the candle must close above it for continuation; wick-through that closes back below its close = rejection block.
- Management of the day's example: four targets stated in advance (minor buy side, hourly level, minor buy side, CE of hourly dynamic range); scale out at the upper quadrant and a limit just below the level (limits at the exact high often do not fill); only ~20% of the original position left by the last stage; optimal trade entry (OTE) long on the first push; stop under the 1-minute consolidation / equilibrium of the consolidation ("last line in the sand for morning-session bullishness"). When the position is long in the tooth, trail the stop tight under the last low ("strangle the trade"), so it runs or stops out in profit.
- Consolidation rule: in a bullish consolidation, price only retraces to the upper half/equilibrium; if it cannot reach equilibrium, bullish. Same PD-array quadrant logic applies to consolidations. (He points to the 2016-17 Core Content first lectures: retracement, expansion, consolidation.)
- Be content with the planned target; do not chase "more" or revenge-trade; decide in advance what you will take and walk away.
- Demo for months before real size; learn to outline the day in advance before taking entries.

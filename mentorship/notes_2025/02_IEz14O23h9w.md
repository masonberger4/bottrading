## Rules in ICT's wording
- Anticipate, do not react: "the largest money that's made in speculation in the marketplace is made on the basis of anticipation, not reaction ... I called this as an inversion fair value gap before it even formed." Rule-based, time-based, "scheduled delivery". [new principle; the 2025 digest has only the narrative form]
- Raid test: price must trade through the old low to show whether it is a raid: "I want to see it travel below it ... it will give me immediate feedback on whether or not this was a raid or if it's going to continue going lower." Below an old low sit "sell stops protecting long holders or sell stop entry as a mode of entering going short". [new]
- Daily/hourly candle opening in close proximity to an old low or high: "This opening price to that candlestick's low. Close proximity ... strong likelihood that it's going to traverse intraday during this daily candle, it's going to trade down to that low. And the way I teach, it's going to reach below that low because there's liquidity below it." Same on an hourly chart: "It matters not what time frame you're trading on." Not an invitation to trade every hour: "I don't want you to feel like you got to trade every hour." [new]
- Smart money reversal (SMR) is the base of every market maker model: "Smart money reversal will always ... be a purge on liquidity." "Model 2022 ... that is low-risk entry. That's the part of the market maker sell model or buy model that you're trading ... Turtle soup. That's the smart money reversal." "Everything is fractal." [new]
- Stages of a market maker buy model (sell is the mirror): SMR (turtle soup) takes the low, then the low-risk entry, then first-stage accumulation, then the retracement into an inefficiency = "second stage reaccumulation ... the unicorn of the models that is the biggest most explosive sustained price run that trades to the opposing liquidity". Unicorn here is the second-stage reaccumulation/redistribution, not an FVG inside a breaker. [new; supports UNICORN B]
- Low-risk entry after the SMR: "If there is no fair value gap over here, the smart money reversal forms the very first bullish fair value gap that forms. That is your low-risk buy. If it's reversed, it's going to be your low-risk sell. Always." And: "that is an inversion fair value gap or first presented fair value gap after the turn or the curve begins. So it's one or the other. If there's an inversion fair value gap there, you use that one. If it's not there, you're going to take the very first fair value gap." He also calls the first drop-and-rip FVG on EURUSD "institutional order flow entry drill". [new; compare FPFVG-DEF]
- The buy program starts when price trades back through the opening price of the candle that took the low: "as soon as that candlestick trades back above the opening price of this candlestick, then we entered technically algorithmically, we're now in a buy program ... the market is now bullish order flow." "Soon as it comes back to opening price and trades one tick above it, you can be long" (the "reverse order block" play on words). All the model's PD arrays "are going to exist inside that range" (from that opening price back up). [new]
- Dealing range and low-hanging fruit: frame the range from the highest high before the drop (an "easy" reactionary high) to the SMR low; "The easiest draw low hanging fruit if you're trading a market maker buy model is 70.5 of the range." Also "three-quarters of the move, or optimal trade entry". "A market maker buy model ... does not have to go all the way back to the original consolidation or opposing liquidity. That's just the best case scenario ... Best case scenarios ... are not an absolute." OTE 62-79%, "70.5 which is the sweet spot". [refines; repeats OTE 70.5 sweet spot in the digest]
- Purge and revert: "Purge and revert is when we take a low out like that ... soon as the low is taken, you go back three days. One, two, three. That high right there. That's the liquidity it's going to draw to ... That's your model. You don't even need it to go back to ... 70.5." Mirror for a high. "Persian [purge and] revert could be your complete model if you're outside of intraday charts using a daily chart ... You won't trade every day." Higher-probability short-term algorithmic target; "not aiming for 70.5". [new]
- "Working class hero" daily-open model (non-intraday): wait for the new daily candle to open in close proximity to the low; "you want to see it trade below it". Put a good-till-cancel buy stop above/at the taken low ("As soon as that low gets taken out, you put a good till cancelled buy stop"); stop at the low "or maybe a little bit below it"; objective 70.5 of the range, or the first swing high from the low as the casual target. Also "It's a very static price entry. It's very, very generic and it works." Can be run on 1-minute or 15-second too. If you use index futures: "decide whether or not you're going to use settlement price or non-settlement price ... always refer to both and back testing and studying". [new]
- Premise vs narrative vs bias: "A premise is not a narrative. Premise is it's probably going to do this ... go up or down once it goes to that level. That's not bias. ... Narrative is price is going to go down below that low because there's sell side. ... It's going to go down there and then now you start using a bias. Once it does that, I'm bullish." [new, definitional]
- Dollar-led forex: "always, if you're not trading with a dollar-based idea, you are trading blind." DXY dropping to its low = buy scalps in pairs with USD second (EURUSD, GBPUSD, AUDUSD); when the low is taken, sell those longs. Pairs with USD first (USDJPY, USDCHF) mirror DXY: short, and close the short when the DXY low is taken. [refines A8 triad]
- SMT on that low: DXY takes its low but "dollar yen ... may not go all the way below" and "Swissy ... might agree and go that slightly lower low"; this divergence "could be indicative of a shift in internals that's going to support ... a larger magnitude reversal". Dow theory is the basis of SMT: "I didn't invent SMT. I just named it ... never settled on what the T stands for." [refines; SMT-USE A]
- SMT among indices: weight S&P > NQ > Dow. "If the diverging average is the Dow, I don't care ... I have lost a lot of money trying to do that." A bullish premise plus a failed low in the S&P while Dow and NQ make lower lows = turtle soup long: "kind of like a loaded deal". Reason: "Dow ... only comprised of 30 stocks ... one stock can cause it to go way up"; NQ is "exaggerated ... thinner than the S&P". [new; SMT-SET B]
- Pair choice without SMT: "if there is no SMT ... look at how much of the range changes are allowed ... the one that has larger more prominent down close candles that's indicating that that's the one that's the weaker one. So the next sell side opportunity ... use the first premium array it forms and get short there." (EURUSD stunted vs GBPUSD ripping lower.) [new; SMT-USE C]
- Bodies vs wicks on gradient levels: "the wicks are allowed to do the damage ... The bodies are allowed to stay in the upper half of a inefficiency" (bullish); wicks "can traverse down below the midpoint ... but it doesn't close below the midpoint". "Consequent encroachment is the midpoint of an inefficiency or gap. If it is a candlestick like this one ... a bullish order block. The middle of that is called mean threshold." [repeats]
- A wick is treated like a gap and measured from the close/open to the extreme: "You measure it ... this closing price down to that low. Here's the lower quadrant level of that wick." Permissible: trade to the upper quadrant and to the CE of the wick, but "We do not want to see the bodies left once the candle closes" under the CE. [repeats L46 wick CE; refines]
- New wick inside an array re-bases the order flow: "as soon as we place one down here like that, we have now limited the order flow in a way where we can see what's permissible and what's not" (next candle's wick to the new wick's lower quadrant to the tick: "diabolical"). There is "an hierarchy" inside the daily bullish FVG. [refines]
- Management cut: "if it drops a body below consequent encroachment of the one of these PD arrays and you hold a position ... cut half the position. Simple." Same on GBPUSD: "collapse half your position and look to another future PDA to reestablish that same portion of your trade." [new; PARTIAL-TRIGGER B]
- Stop trail: after entry, stop sits below the SMR low; once a new swing low forms and the candle closes, "your stop loss can be brought up right below that low". Second-stage reaccumulation/redistribution "never gets a stop hunt on it. Never. If it does, you're on the wrong side ... you want to be out." [new; STOP-MGMT A]
- Which wick to grade: the lowest-reaching (for lows) / highest-reaching (for highs) among neighbours, not simply the longest: "it's not simply the length of the wick, it's how far the wick is reaching lower. Like this one's definitely lower than that one." [new; see New conflicts WICK-SELECT]
- FVG formed on a wick (gold): "if it's formed with wicks ... And then if there's a wick to the left of that that is in the same range ... this wick is not the importance. This wick is the importance. So you want to grade that." Quadrant hits to 0.1 point on gold daily (4249.9 vs 4250; 4215.1 vs 4214.0). [new; WICK-PRIORITY B]
- Inversion FVG creation and validation: "That close right there, above the inversion fair value gap ... Bang validates. It will be an inversion fair value gap." Premise-based expectation: price took sell side, comes back up through the gap, "this right here will always behave as an inversion fair value." Vocabulary: "inversion", not "inverse". [refines; IFVG-CONFIRM A and see New conflicts IFVG-ANTICIPATE]
- Volume imbalance to SIBI/BISI: a VI (body-to-body gap between two candles with no wick-to-wick void) after a down leg becomes "sellside imbalance buyside inefficiency" (sell side delivered, buy side missing); price then "rolls right back up" like a paint roller; once both are delivered it is "a balanced price range ... very stiff real resistance". If price trades below an old SIBI/BISI and returns it acts "much in the same way as an inversion fair value". "Common gap" (no kiss, wick-to-wick void) = "vacuum block"; "liquidity void" is only the span where no trading occurred. "We cut through candles. We don't look at liquidity voids." [refines]
- Relative equal lows validation: "The most recent low with relative equal lows, it needs to be higher than the one you're anchored to ... The one slightly higher is a validation that this is likely to be attacked." Graded wick on it: want a body close below the wick CE ("speed to close below this halfway point") to confirm the run; "not that it needs to close below that for the short to be valid" but these "build confidence". [new; LIQ-LOCATION C]

## Rules in ICT's wording
- Called-in-advance level delivered: the DXY SIBI (daily) was the level the market would come back to "and not reach the midpoint and trade into the lower half" and then "it hits it perfectly ... uses the top of it here, shows discount sensitivity, and then starts to rally." "The algorithm is going to keep price in the upper half and not trade in the lower half." [repeats]
- Wicks: "every wick we treat like a gap. And how do we treat gaps? ... We grade them: the high, upper quadrant, consequent encroachment, lower quadrant, and the low. And no, that is not quarters theory ... It's quadrants on gaps and inefficiencies. Wicks are just like a gap." [repeats L46 / L23]
- Choice among adjacent premium wicks: he used the one that reaches higher: "This one's longer than this one ... it's slightly higher ... if I'm going to be using any gradients on a wick? This one. It's the higher reaching one." [new; see New conflicts]
- Premium wick as extension past equal highs: "this premium wick is part of this sell-side balance buy-side inefficiency trading above this because we have relative equal highs. So what would be the next PD array that would go above that? Well, you're going to look at the wick." Price rallied to the wick high (97.823), then to the wick's upper quadrant (97.861), "fall short of reaching the consequent encroachment, but leaves relative equal highs." Bodies respected the wick low (97.649) and gathered in a FVG. [new]
- Overlapping quadrants go onto the 1-minute chart: "an overlapping of these quadrants that need to be transposed into your lower time frame charts." [repeats]
- Supercharge rule: "If you see a quadrant or a PD array that has gradient levels in it, if that is sitting right with a PD array like a fair value gap, an order block, a breaker, it supercharges the likelihood that it's going to be very sensitive. And if you apply time, what time of day, what day of week, is it a macro time, it further increases the likelihood." No promise of profit: management and psychology are separate from reading. [repeats; supports L04 FVG-on-quadrant]
- Time windows for forex: "Here's London open kill zone, New York open kill zone and the London close." "It's kind of like CAPS day for forex. I don't really like to look at anything in the afternoon hours post noon time Eastern time for forex or dollar index ... generally there's really no real interesting price moves for me after 12:00 noon" except rate announcements. "I'd be focusing on index futures [in the afternoon]." [new; forex window = London open to noon]
- Institutional order flow entry drill: bullish FVG, price "goes in just a little bit, but fails to even go to halfway or consequent encroachment." Repeated twice, then a FVG that accumulates around CE and hits the high. [refines; differs from L46, see New conflicts]
- Unicorn (his usage): premium wick low hit (97.649) then "the upper quadrant of that premium wick and now it's a fair value gap touching it as well and it's also during a macro time ... you got another unicorn in your repertoire." Wick-quadrant + FVG + macro time = "unicorn". [new; see Scorecard UNICORN]
- Orange rectangle convention: a SIBI-style rectangle anchored on an up-close candle, "orange" = price is below it, "I'm expecting this to act as an inversion fair value gap." EURUSD broke down through it ("an inversion fair value gap") on a higher dollar; "I said I was bullish [dollar] until it proves by going down lower through an inversion fair value gap." [repeats]
- Breakaway gap definition: "consolidation of a higher time frame PD. So any gap left there, that's an easy way of classifying it as a breakaway gap." and "Look for gaps that are inside consolidations or midpoints [CE] of higher time frame PD arrays." Put these in the journal. [new]
- Sequence in EURUSD: London open kill zone gap + IOFED, accumulates shorts around the CE of the inversion FVG, SIBI, then FVG, then an old green inefficiency twice ("it's not classic support and resistance, it's trading inside of an inefficiency during the New York open kill zone"), immediate rebalance, then the high of the discount wick and its lower quadrant. - Cable measuring gap: "this is a measuring gap. It never filled it in again ... measuring gaps always about halfway [of the move]"; the bearish order block is the change of the state of delivery; "the opening price of an up-close candle. It was rallying here. The algorithm changes gears and anything at that price or lower it will spool and reach for lower discount or sell side." Inefficiency by the discount wick: "we trade down low, hit the high of it, trade a lower quadrant, come back up to the high, hit the order block." [refines]

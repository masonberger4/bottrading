Full read (2.8k words). Core model rule set for 2025 (NQ, 1-minute chart).
## Rules in ICT's wording
1. **Definition.** Chart toggled to **regular trading hours (RTH)**, New York time. The opening range gap is the gap between the previous day's RTH settlement (last 1-minute candle close, 4:14 p.m. ET, e.g. 21497) and the next day's first RTH open at 9:30 ET. Students wrongly think the new day opening gap is the ORG; with ETH on you see new day/new week opening gaps instead, not ORGs.
2. **Premium / discount.** Gap up at 9:30 vs 4:14 close = premium ORG; gap down = discount ORG. Only matters for FIB direction.
3. **FIB.** Anchor at the 4:14 settlement, drag to the 9:30 open (up for premium, down for discount). Settings: quadrant levels (0, 0.25 lower quadrant, 0.5 CE, 0.75 upper quadrant, 1). The gap is a "liquidity void" in RTH (no trading in it; "unfortunately classified" as a void since ETH has trading).
4. **By size (NQ handles = points):**
 - **Over ~120 handles (extreme gap):** probably keeps going in the gap direction. If it trades back, it usually stops at the **upper quadrant** (25% of the gap from the open end) (premium case) and may not enter the gap or fill it for days; later eventually fills ("patchwork"). Leery of fading it.
 - **20 to 75 handles:** likes the odds of trading to **mid-gap (CE)**; for a premium gap: first run buy side to a pre-market (6:00-9:30 ET) or London high / relative equal highs, then trade back to at least CE (minimum), possibly to full closure depending on bias or range.
 - **75 to 120 handles:** gray area, "can go either direction"; lean on other analysis: economic calendar, seasonal tendencies, current market structure; may sit on hands until 10:00 and not trade the 9:30-10:00 opening range.
5. Levels from a prior ORG (high, UQ, CE, LQ, low) are reused the next days (the market delivered to them over two sessions). Highlight every ORG on the chart. Smooth, clean relative equal highs/lows from them get disrupted ("smooth edges made jagged").

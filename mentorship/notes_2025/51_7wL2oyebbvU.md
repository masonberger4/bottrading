## Rules in ICT's wording
**Narrative frame (Venom, "how to frame a narrative"):**
- Look at the electronic trading hours (pre-RTH) range from **8:00 to 9:30 ET, a "very specific 90-minute window"**. "Where's the high and where's the low inside that range of time." The high is your buy side, the low is your sell side. "It's not ambiguous." (He denies this is the 90-minute cycles teaching; it is a lookback window that sets the algorithm's first frame of reference for the opening range.)
- These are "initial frames of reference" for the open and the move into 10:00-10:30, "that first hour of trading ... really the whole basis of what I've been doing for 2025." Price need not go only to those or stop there.
- Bias comes from the weekly/daily draw: "if you expect the market to be predominantly bullish or bearish for the week then only focus on the buy side in that realm"; bullish means look at the sell side in the 90-minute window at 9:30. "You're going to miss some trades": sometimes it does not go to your high or low and starts running; then use the opening range gap, consequent encroachment, or the Silver Bullet.
- Bearish day: the market "makes its protraction to the high first" (the 8:00-9:30 high; relative equal highs) to take buy stops, so smart money sells into them and anchors shorts; then it gravitates to the 8:00-9:30 low ("lowest low in the 90-minute lookback at 9:30").
- Context requirement: the market must be predisposed to one direction. "If it's in the middle of a trading range and has done several large range candles up or down, Venom will not appear." Venom "unseats those that would otherwise be profitable."
**Venom signature (bearish version):**
1. A candle shoots above the relative equal highs (the raid on buy side) and **closes above** them. It must be an inefficient run higher into it (a gap, i.e. imbalance, as it goes up).
2. The next candle breaks down and **closes back below**, leaving a second imbalance. "Imbalance here, imbalance here": these are "the two fangs," the telltale signs. The up-close candle is left inside the pool of liquidity "and then quickly left."
3. This candle's closing price is "the change in the state of delivery." Treated as "a real turtle soup" (not the Street Smarts 20-day turtle soup; "light years beyond that").
**Entry options (bearish; mirror for bullish):**
- Highest form / high-risk: sell short ABOVE the relative equal highs as price runs into them (needs great conviction).
- Low-risk: wait for the retrace. Either "institutional wonder entry ... about this candle's high" (the Venom candle's high / its open region) or the **opening price of the last candle, "the Venom order block,"** which is the change in the state of delivery (price may go "two more ticks to make sure the price is there" then run).
- Failing that, he used first presented FVG after the raid (deferred entry model): enter inside the first presented fair value gap that forms after the run.
- Other confirmation he used: price traded up toward but **fell short of the new day opening gap low** ("if it can't touch that, is it weak? Yes").
- Zero drawdown on entry in the example; he took a manual exit at the ETH (pre-market) sell side liquidity, a limit order sat lower and price went about 10-15 handles beyond.
**Opening range gap (OPG) context:** after an extreme gap lower (here ~1,100 points) price likely will NOT even trade back to consequent encroachment (normally "generally a 70% likelihood" within the first 30 minutes); it may trade back into the gap but "modestly." Open range = 9:30-10:00. The new day opening gap (6 p.m. open to prior settlement) in an underlying weak market is "pretty much the extreme of the retracement into the opening range gap." Quadrants (upper, lower, CE) drawn on it. The 9:30 opening price is extended as a level and price "respects or misses" it.
**Inversion FVG and power of three:** a big candle with a volume imbalance between bodies counts as part of the inefficiency (sell side balance buy side efficiency). When bearish and price trades down through it, it becomes an inversion FVG; price traded above the opening price (power of three "not some garbage candle range theory") and entered "algorithmically." Weakness: price fails to reach the inversion FVG's consequent encroachment, then trades down.
**Lunch macro (same day):** 11:30-1:30 New York lunch period (2 hours). "At 10 o'clock start walking forward, where's the high that forms when you're bearish": that high is the lunch macro target; the market gravitates to it from 11:30, used the inversion FVG (now reclaimed order block), cleared volume imbalances and buy side, "anyone short on the day is not going to be allowed to take a ride below the morning low."
**PM session:** 1:30-2:00 p.m. = opening range for PM session. First presented FVG when bearish acts as inversion FVG. Wait for the **PM macro 2:50-3:10**: price trades up into the gap (respect the bodies), then the first gap that forms after is the entry, selling off through the morning-session low. Measurement: "the lowest closer open to the highest closer open in that range" (bodies, not wicks) for the range height.
**Daily chart close:** he used the big-wick candle's CE (17,745) as the retracement level so a deep retrace that bumps but does not take out the low suffices; taking out that low is "first partial." (Price levels unresolved.)

Backtest, journal, forward test; don't blend his material with others; "a model is just taking components I've already taught and putting them in a context that makes sense."

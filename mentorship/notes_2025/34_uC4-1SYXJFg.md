## Rules in ICT's wording
**Weekly outlook method (no opening price known):**
- Weekly chart: premium wick; its consequent encroachment (CE); lower quadrant graded; bullish draw = the old CIBI level; "trying to avoid picking a top." If price gets above the black top line of the premium wick (bounce point), continuation to all-time highs.
- Daily: a gap "needs to stay open" (price coming all the way back down into it is problematic); otherwise range between the weekly SIBI low and that gap.
- On Sunday you do not know the weekly open: "Nobody knows where the new week opening price is going to be ... speculating on the initial bias isn't 100%." "You have to submit yourself to 6:00 Eastern time, see where the open price, then weigh your scenarios." Not plan A/plan B to cheer later; a trading plan for how to engage depending on the open. Gap-down scenario: look for the low and the sell side below it; to stay bullish do not want to see price below that low. Geopolitical events can cause out-of-left-field gaps.
**First presented FVG (AM, 1-minute chart), "model taught my son Caleb":**
- The FVG that forms on the 1-minute chart between 9:31 and 10:00 (first presented). Extend it right through the day and week; it is used "over and over and over." Label them, keep them organized.
- He used the 9:31 first FVG (not a later one) because the criteria: opening was a large premium gap that raided the previous RTH relative equal highs; "this is your first presented fair value gap because it's done a raid on previous RTH relative equal highs." (Caleb learned by experience when to use it; ICT does not give a full rule list here.)
- Draw the quadrant gradient: high, upper quadrant, CE, lower quadrant, low. "We're staying outside the upper half. That's order flow theory with my PD arrays. It's always like that."
- A FVG changes character: initially a sell side imbalance buy side inefficiency used to sell off to the target; once the target is reached and price trades above it, it becomes bullish (respect CE; falls short of CE, comes back down into it). Conversely a buy-side imbalance FVG that is traded below flips (CE of it sends price down). "Reversal of what its original character was."
- PM session: first presented FVG between 1:30 and 2:00 (the PM opening range); price trades down to the low of the AM first presented FVG, rallies above CE, accumulates in the first resistive FVG 1:30-2:00. "It helps you identify the actual fair value gaps. That's going to help you know which one to trust ... never fall victim to picking the wrong fair value gap."
**Opening range gap (RTH):** measure from previous settlement (4:14 p.m. RTH; he also says 4:15 is the settlement) to the 9:30 open; "soon as we get the opening price, you know you're looking for 70% likelihood it's going to pull back into that opening range gap 50% of it" (CE). Minor buy side liquidity sits near it. Professional measure: where do we open vs where do we close, per session (AM 9:30 to ~11:00, PM from the first tick at 1:30 to 4:00), not retail previous-close-to-open.
**Blend RTH and ETH structure:** read RTH relative equal highs/lows together with ETH liquidity. Time first: from 9:30 going back, first run into London session liquidity, then Asia session liquidity ("London's low, Asia's low ... two levels to look for"). He called "run the buy stops, now looking for 21,360 based on London low" in a student's stream. Trading canvas option: pre-market 7:00 (he says 7:00 to 9:30, a 90-minute window) can be your entire trading canvas; or use a session as a buffer then trade heavier after the opening range.
**Day structure (his liquidity cycle):** Asia, London, pre-market 7:00-9:30 highs and lows; the 9:30-11:00 AM session uses that liquidity; the lunch macro "pulls against that" (consolidation, reversal or continuation back into the range formed ~10:00); the PM session uses the lunch macro and/or AM liquidity. "That cycle repeats."
**Standard-deviation target:** measure the opening range high to low and project -1.5 standard deviation on the fib; that level took price below London and Asia liquidity (Friday). Trades down into a buy side imbalance sell side inefficiency "right there" without needing to take the low.
**Other levels used:** Friday London and Asia session liquidity; Thursday RTH liquidity; Tuesday PM session first FVG (candle bodies respected); Thursday's 60-minute premium wick CE (the 50% of the wick of the highest 60-minute candle); 3:15-3:45 macro; "model 2022" after a shift in market structure and FVG break lower to the PM first FVG low.
**Process rules:** Do not use level two, DOM, volume profile, time-and-sales: spoofing; "You only need one contract to cause the price to create the candlestick ... Markets don't always turn on big volume." One algorithm per instrument ("price engine"). Futures are a "gentleman's market" with same highs/lows at all brokers (not CFD/forex). Everything stems from time, delivery, then price. Keep a naked chart; take annotations off periodically so as not to force an outcome; use templates and toggles; every couple minutes check the last five new day opening gaps, new week opening gaps, Asian session liquidity from earlier in the week. Weekend prep: 30 to 60 minutes reviewing where turning points occurred, how the weekly high/low relates to weekly and monthly charts. Specialize in one or two PD arrays; let a missed setup go. Learn month four content of the Core Content (PD array matrix). Entries need not be at the high/low: use continuation PD arrays when not at the screen.

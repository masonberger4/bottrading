## Rules in ICT's wording
- No level 2, footprint, DOM ladders, depth of market, VWAP, volume profile, POC, Ichimoku, Elliott, supply/demand: "the algorithm doesn't see your stop, doesn't refer to number of contracts"; it uses only time and open/high/low/close. Bias (draw on liquidity) + OHLC + time of day/session "is all that you need."
- Daily chart is back and forth unless in a fast low resistance liquidity run.
- DXY: gap down used as an inversion FVG to work toward sell-side; a bullish FVG that price trades down through then back above becomes a "reclaimed bullish FVG." He does not forget filled inefficiencies: "the algorithm isn't going to forget about it." Reclaimed FVG that fails to trade to its CE = "institutional orderflow entry drill" (a failed CE test) = confluence with inversion FVG lower quadrant and bodies respecting.
- Sell-side imbalance buy-side inefficiency (SIBI) is a bearish-classified FVG: one long down-close candle, with next candle's high below previous candle's low. Premium/discount: the lower half is the discount array, upper half premium.
- Price Delivery Continuum / cycling: at every new 60-minute open check the hourly chart (inefficiencies, respected?, aiming for buy or sell side); every 15-minute close refer to the 15-min; every 5-min close refer to the 5-min; then return to the 1-min. "It's not top-down analysis, it's cycling through continuously." Not living on the higher timeframes.
- Bearish FVG: study its UPPER half on the lower timeframe first (bullish: the lower half). Back-and-forth delivery in the upper half = balanced price range (efficient), so no need for price to return to fill it; it then trades to the lower half and validates it. The remaining true inefficiency is the lower half (only a single pass of sell delivery), which price must offer back up to the midpoint. Delivery up to the midpoint "satisfies the coding"; need not go higher.
- A 15-minute FVG can look different on 5-min/1-min (3 one-minute candles make one 15-min). The 5-min FVG formed in the lower part is the "real" one.
- Timing: one FVG forms every 15-minute interval on the 15 or 5-min chart (10:00-10:15, 10:15-10:30, ...) whenever there is a draw on liquidity: four per hour, "bus route every 15 minutes." If no FVGs materialize you are in high resistance liquidity run conditions: sit still 15 more minutes, repeat to session end; if the whole session is high resistance, no trade, return next day. A FVG need not be traded in the interval it formed; wait for the one where everything lines up.
- Stop placement: the upper half of a bearish 15-min FVG that will not fill (breakaway gap) lets the stop sit in the upper quadrant or just above the shaded area high (more conservative). CE is "a little too close and the spread might get you"; wicks are allowed to do damage. If stopped out and price trades back into the lower half, re-enter short with half the contracts (stop again upper quadrant / above high).
- Bias logic: toward buy-side draw look for bullish FVGs or bearish FVGs that fail and become inversion FVGs.
- Macro 10:50-11:10: trade into the lower half of the 15-min FVG; consecutive up-close candles bearish order block ("not a supply zone" = change in state of delivery, lowest open of the consecutive candles); breakaway gap, then measuring gap (measured move, about halfway).
- Avoid NFP ("not for professionals").
- Learning: journal, log, back test, then tape-read live; no demo, no funded accounts until then.

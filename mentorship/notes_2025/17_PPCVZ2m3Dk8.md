- Why an intermediate-term high was due: "when it's breaking out to all new all-time highs and it's doing it in a lot of strength, leaving inefficiencies open, not coming back down into the volume imbalance ... it just keeps powering higher. ... if you were a brand new trader, you would just chase that going long ... that's usually indicative of when a blowoff type move or an intermediate term high is likely to form because the public seeing this ... Street money ... want to accumulate whatever long position they can and just hold on." [new; ATH blowoff context for intermediate high, relates ATH-RULE A]
- Bias on daily, structure on the 1-minute: "we're using the daily chart for bias and direction and key sensitive levels, but the structure is inside of the intraday chart ... the one minute chart because that's where the intermediate term high is going to form." [repeats L77 top-down; BIAS-SOURCE A]
- Draw after an intermediate high: sell-side liquidity and the daily premium wick. "Classic support and resistance would say just take that old high draw that out in time and when it trades down there it should bounce. Well it didn't do that did it? So we want to look inside these inefficiencies because they're just like gaps ... the upper quadrant and consequent encroachment ... defines whether or not this is going to be an attenuation of a deeper retracement or just coming back down in here to consolidate and send it even higher." [repeats]
- Larry Williams swing definition: "Intermediate term high is any high that has two lower short-term highs to the right and left of it ... it's fractal ... very simplistic, very easy, it's visual. I would admit that there is some measure of subjectivity to it, but over time and experience, you'll see how to use the right swing highs and swing lows." [repeats L26/L23; 3-bar short-term high chain]
- Wait for the break, do not anticipate the run: relative equal highs into 09:30 "you might expect it to run up there and wipe out the buy side and then break lower. You have to wait. You have to wait for it to just tear off and go lower." Break of relative equal lows = shift in market structure. I like to use the more pronounced, the obvious displacement one ... it can be one tick, but I'm going to be more likely to pick an inefficiency that's much more protracted.

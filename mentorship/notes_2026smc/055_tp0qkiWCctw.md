## Summary
Wednesday opened with an enormous premium ORG, running into last Wednesday's noon 1H BISI again. ICT shorted 1, then 3 contracts, aiming for relative equal lows and then the ORG CE with 17 minutes left before 10:00 (70%). Price chopped hard ("high resistance liquidity run"): stops, re-entries, partials taken out of "concern", stop eventually hit near relative equal highs. Net small. The ORG CE was not reached during the session window. Main teaching: how to recognise high- vs low-resistance liquidity run conditions and the "bodies vs wicks" tell at a target low.
## Rules in ICT's wording
- **Morning session bias (simplest)**: "between 9:30 and 11 o'clock or 10:30, where did we gap? Did we gap higher or gap lower? Find the mid gap. There's your target. Aim for that." On a big premium gap, "I only want to stick on one side."
- ORG CE by 10:00: "Got about 17 minutes till 10:00. So, 70% likelihood that we'll draw back to half gap."
- Premium array test: "I want to see it fail to get in the upper half, like hit it, then start to break down."
- **SIBI / wick defence**: keep bodies out of the upper half of the SIBI; "Touching that ... is okay. I don't want to see it leave a body up there." Long-wick CE: "I'd prefer not to have a body above that."
- Manipulation tell: a wick through the gap's CE whose body does not lay on the CE = "manual intervention ... running against anyone that's short."
- **Bodies vs wicks at a target low (continuation test)**: "if they can bury bodies below that low you're looking for to break through, that's a confirmation. A retracement or a reversal is signified by" candles keeping bodies at and above the low with only wicks below. "The bodies are showing the narrative," even if wick-to-wick a level was taken.
- **High resistance liquidity run (HRLR)**: "Lots of price action shared in a few number of candlesticks" = "very manipulated"; you can be stopped and still see the idea deliver. "This is what I teach my students not to participate in." Paid mentorship focus: identify it, "move to the sidelines ... paper trade it ... but don't try to trade in it with your live funds."
- **Low resistance liquidity run (LRLR)**: "one or two candles in a range ... stretching out like taffy." Engage then.
- LRLR is promoted by blending "economic calendar, market structure, day of week, time of day, the very specific draw on liquidity that's obvious on the daily, obvious on the 4 hour, obvious on the 1 hour chart."
- Obvious stops get run quickly, not gradually.
- **Trade management**: "as soon as that thought jumps in your head [to exit], the first thing you do is take something off." Keep size low on such days; know when you'll stop. Take one partial under the relative equal lows, then trail the stop above the candle high.
- After two stops in HRLR: "I'm not going to pursue anything else the rest of the morning."
- Shared stop publicly = it got run (anecdote; ignore for engine).
- When it fails to launch and leaves wicks below the low, expect a bullish FVG reclaim and a run on trailed stops above.

## Rules in ICT's wording
- "All inefficiencies need to be graded." Daily or weekly inefficiencies: when trading inside one, its octants and quadrants matter. Any FVG, OB, breaker, VI, wick or CE that forms on these levels, "when they agree with time and your bias [and] draw on liquidity", is a key PD array.
- **Fib anchor on a body**: use the *higher* of open/close when the array's edge is a body, "the higher one is what it's going to reach down for." For the ORG: the 09:30 candle's **open** to "the last print ... on regular trading hours" (16:14 Friday). "It is the only two reference points you really need ... Listen to the old man" (re students disputing 16:14).
- First presented FVG: the 09:32 SIBI-type gap (VI included). "Its first utilization is ... sell side delivery," so wait for price to go back down through it and close below, then short the retest.
- "Candlestick number two that creates the inefficiency needs to be connected to an octant or a gradient level within a range that is your analysis leading to a draw on liquidity." If you only use the ORG, the draw is full gap closure, and "you don't need to even confer with the daily" array.
- IFVG with a VI between close and open: a bullish up-close gap becomes bearish once price trades below. It traded up to the upper octant "and then falls out of bed."
- **Touching two levels**: candle 2 touching both an upper quadrant and a lower octant means "significant contraction lower" if bearish.
- **Breakaway gap at CE**: "a gap that forms ... exactly around the midpoint [CE] of your analysis leading to an unrealized dealing range ... could become what? A breakaway gap. Would you want to see it traded back into ...? No."
- Suspension-block check: verify VI on both ends from open/close prices; without the VI it is "not a suspension block."
- Last inefficiency sat on the ORG low (Friday 16:14 settlement). Price retraced to its CE and stopped, "bodies ... staying below it."
- High probability: "the more things that agree on one side of the market, the less likely it is to go the other direction."

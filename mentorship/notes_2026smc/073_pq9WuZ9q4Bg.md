## Rules in ICT's wording
- **Suspension block grading**: short "at the daily suspension block octant levels"; "used the lower quadrant of the daily suspension block. Premium sensitivity came in at a time of the day [macro 09:50-10:10]. It should create that false breakout."
- **Entry grading**: prefer to short the high candle; "the bodies didn't go any higher than my entry candle" = acceptable.
- **IFVG while short**: "I don't want to see that inversion fair value gap fail ... No more bodies in the upper half." Low of the IFVG never retraded to = "very weak market."
- **Wick CE sensitivity (central teaching)**: bullish case: "Watch the wick. You have to measure that because it's going to act with discount sensitivity ... the upper half to consequent encroachment. It can touch it. It can't put a body below it ... that invalidates the bullishness." Not touching CE at all validates strength. Bearish mirror: "We don't want any bodies above [the wick's CE] ... that's premium sensitivity you have to allow those wicks to do that."
- **OB change in state of delivery (CISD) entry**: "this candlestick at 9:49 ... Look at the opening price ... It trades above that opening price ... Once it trades above, it does not need to close above it." Premise: running from a discount to a premium after hitting a HTF PDA. Retrace to the exact opening price = entry ("That's not a demand zone ... It's a very specific price").
- **Series of down-close candles**: the OB is the down-close candle with the largest body in the series (PM 14:06 example; low traded 3 ticks into its open).
- **Entry/stop frame**: long "anywhere from [candle close] to the consequent encroachment" of the wick, stop = "the low of that wick because it shouldn't go below consequent encroachment."
- **Next candle opens below the CISD opening price** then trades into it = "just go in there and take a long."
- **FPFVG choice**: 09:35 gap preferred over an earlier one "because look how much more prominent that run is."
- **FVG validation**: "The fair value gaps are either first presentation, and if they're not first presented, then they are always anchored to a octant or a quadrant. Period." Not volume profile / low-volume nodes. Anchored = "it has to be touching" the graded level (high, low or anywhere in between).
- **Shadow** = overlap of two imbalances (daily bearish FVG with the daily suspension block); grade the overlap itself too. [B2 shadow]
- Sixteenths: split octant-to-quadrant in half "you might want to start doing that." [new research]
- **MOC**: last minutes of the last hour; "Market on close is very algorithmic"; inversion FVG used for MOC.
- **ORG (Larry Williams aside)**: gap higher = premium opening range gap; "if it doesn't fill it all the way, half of it's enough to see it go higher if it's going to be bullish"; bearish = break through and use the ORG to measure projections lower. [refines B2 Caleb ORG model]
- **Two-sided = no trade**: "If you can frame it on both sides of the market, if it can be bullish or bearish and you can argue both sides, don't trade it." "Turn your chart off."
- **July doldrums**: "Slow down in your trades. Look for one or two setups for the week. Once you get it, be done." [same as 070]
- Post-macro run: Tuesday PM low at 14:06 "running after a macro" (13:50-14:10).

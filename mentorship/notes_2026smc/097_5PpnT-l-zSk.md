## Rules in ICT's wording
- **Premium sensitivity**: "Premium sensitivity is where the market uses a PD array or gap and it only involves the lower half of it when you're bearish. It can wick into the upper half, but the bodies are telling you something"; discount sensitivity is the mirror. [B3 wick CE body filter]
- **Between two FVGs is not no man's land**: "because we were between those two fair value gaps, I was basically using that as an eyeballed rough estimate for event horizon"; he started the short above the lower FVG "because I could have just simply got the wick outside of the lower and they never would have traded into" the upper one, then added in the upper one.
- **Mohawk + breakaway gap**: "we wicked through it here. So, that's a mohawk. That's always permissible ... perfect scenario, we want to leave some of that gap open cuz it can act as ... a breakaway gap."
- **After breaking a wick**: "Once we broke through on the downside, I didn't want to see any bodies come back above that"; bodies stay "from midpoint consequent [encroachment] to the low."
- **Carry forward proven FVGs**: "Once the price has utilized the fair value gap with its initial utilization ... those are the ones you want to carry forward in your chart. And then if price can get above that, you would look to see if it can act" as an inversion. Weaker ones (less "detail") get less weight. In back-testing, annotate gaps that proved themselves even if you weren't watching. [refines B3 first utilization]
- **IFVG validation needs a body outside, then a return**: "We just wicked outside, a small little body, but that's not validating the inversion fair value gap. It's got to go outside of it and then come back in ... It's got to leave a body outside of it." [refines B3 3-step IFVG validation]
- **Failed inversion -> back to first utilization**: "with the failure to get above this in a way where it was using it as inversion fair value gap, this gap can return back to its first utilization."
- **Bodies are the real pool**: "sell side is below this block of price action here because the body, see all those bodies? That's the real liquidity pool. It's not so much the wicks"; "look for the run below the bodies here. The wicks, not so much." [new; conflict check: 2023 liquidity at wick extremes]
- **One body beyond the prior day's ORG**: "that one little body below the opening range gap from yesterday, that would be a catalyst for me to anticipate lower prices. But because we have all these other things [Fed] ... it makes it difficult to trust that."
- **Fed speech = low probability**: "we're in very very low probability for precision right now because we're up against 10:00 Fed. Anytime the Fed comes out and gives any kind of testimony ... those speeches ... could cause a lot of damage quickly." [refines 091 FOMC protocol]
- **Order block candle-selection rule ("book definition")**: "If it's a series of candlesticks that are up close, if the last candlestick is the smallest, I'm going to use the opening price on that one. If it's the largest of the series, I'm going to use the opening price on that one. But if they're all uniform ... I'm going to use the very first one of the lowest candlestick in the series." Two equal-bodied candles: "I like the opening price on that [earlier one]." [conflict: B3 A3 "largest body in a series"; conflict check: 2023 OB definition]
- **No clear draw = trade smaller swings or nothing**: "You have to have a clear draw ... if you don't have that, you have to break it down into smaller little price swings ... or just say I'll come back to it and do back testing." A 5-30 second run from an IFVG "might be your model."
- **Later-day expectation**: return to the CE of today's RTH ORG ("whether it's a lunch macro, whether it runs there now or later in the afternoon"), then the run below the bodies.
